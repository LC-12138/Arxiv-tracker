Exceptional cubics on 7-manifolds
Authors: Sergey Grigorian
First: 2026-11-16T18:55:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-16T18:55:34+00:00
Comments: 115 pages, 2 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A $G_2$-structure $\varphi$ on a $7$-manifold $\mathcal M$ determines a two-dimensional space of invariant totally symmetric cubic forms on each fiber of the rank-$27$ bundle $S^2_0T^*\mathcal M$ of traceless symmetric $2$-tensors. This space is spanned by the trace cubic $C_0$ and a cubic $C_\varphi$ that, up to a constant factor, is the pointwise integrand of the Yukawa coupling on the $G_2$ moduli space. We determine the exact infinitesimal stabilizers over $\mathbb C$ of all nonzero cubics in this family. For $C_0+λC_\varphi$, the stabilizer has dimension $78$ precisely at the two non-real values $λ_\pm=(1\pm i\sqrt7)/8$. Its dimension is $21$ for $C_0$ and $14$ in all other cases. At $λ_\pm$, the stabilizer is simple of type $E_6$, and the corresponding cubic is linearly equivalent to the complex Albert algebra cubic norm. The two cubics at $λ_\pm$, which we call critical, define complex-conjugate $E_6$-reductions of the complexified bundle. At any element with nonzero critical norm, the corresponding cubic determines a complex Albert algebra with basepoint stabilizer $F_4$; a line subbundle of the tangent bundle selects a smooth bundle of these algebras with tensorial operations and a Euclidean real form. The induced Levi-Civita connection preserves either critical cubic exactly when the $G_2$-structure is torsion-free. Its orthogonal projections onto the two exceptional reductions coincide with the connection induced by the minimal $G_2$-connection.

The geometry of magnitude for finite metric spaces
Authors: Karel Devriendt
First: 2025-10-16T13:50:37+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-16T18:48:29+00:00
Comments: 18 pages, 2 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The main result of this article is a geometric interpretation of magnitude, a real-valued invariant of metric spaces. We introduce a Euclidean embedding of a (suitable) finite metric space $X$ such that the magnitude of $X$ can be expressed in terms of the `circumradius' of its embedding $S$. The circumradius is the radius of the unique sphere that goes through $S$. We give three applications: First, we describe the asymptotic behaviour of the magnitude of $tX$ as $t\rightarrow \infty$, in terms of the circumradius. Second, we develop a matrix theory for magnitude that leads to explicit relations between the magnitude of $X$ and the magnitude of its subspaces. Third, we identify a new regime in the limiting behaviour of $tX$, and use this to show submodularity-type results for magnitude as a function on subspaces.

On strongly pseudoconvex Kähler--Berwald manifolds
Authors: Xiaoshu Ge, Xiangyu Zhou
First: 2026-11-16T18:43:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-16T18:43:59+00:00
Comments: 48 pages, no figures. The core results in the Kähler--Berwald setting were first announced at the Young Mathematicians Workshop on Several Complex Variables, Incheon, Republic of Korea, August 2026, when the equivalence between the Kähler--Berwald and Kähler--Finsler conditions had not yet been established
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In this paper, we establish the equivalence between the Kähler--Finsler and Kähler--Berwald conditions for complex Finsler metrics. That is, a Kähler--Finsler metric always satisfies the complex Berwald condition: its Chern--Finsler nonlinear connection reduces to the Chern connection of a Kähler metric. We also provide, for the first time, smooth weakly Kähler--Finsler examples which are not Kähler--Berwald; and propose a condition that is formally stronger than weakly Kähler--Finsler, with equivalence holding in complex dimension two. Then we prove the de Rham decomposition and uniformization theorems for Kähler--Berwald manifolds, and show that every strongly pseudoconvex locally symmetric complex Finsler metric is both Kähler--Berwald and complex Einstein--Finsler. Additionally, we establish several rigidity theorems for the Kobayashi and Carathéodory metrics: $F_K$ is Kähler--Berwald if and only if it is Kähler; if the manifold is simply connected, then $F_C$ is Kähler--Berwald if and only if it is Kähler.

Fourier Algebras of HLS Groupoids: Lifts, Amenability, and Spectrum
Authors: Joseph DeGaetani, Mahya Ghandehari
First: 2026-11-16T18:43:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-16T18:43:47+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We investigate the Fourier algebra of a certain class of groupoids, called the HLS groupoids, within Paterson's continuous framework for locally compact groupoids. We develop the basic harmonic analysis of HLS groupoids by obtaining concrete descriptions of their topology, representations, and Fourier algebra. We then study the restriction maps from the Fourier algebra $A(\mathcal{G})$ of an HLS groupoid $\mathcal{G}$ to the Fourier algebras of its isotropy subgroups, and construct explicit norm-preserving lifts for these restriction maps. As applications, we describe the norm of every element of $A(\mathcal{G})$ in terms of the norms of its restrictions to the isotropy subgroups. We prove an analogue of Leptin's theorem characterizing when $A(\mathcal{G})$ has a bounded approximate identity. We also show that the Gelfand spectrum of $A(\mathcal{G})$ is homeomorphic to $\mathcal{G}$. Finally, using abstract arguments that, unlike our methods for HLS groupoids, do not yield explicit constructions, we show that norm-preserving lifts exist and that the Gelfand spectrum of the Fourier algebra is homeomorphic to the groupoid for every étale groupoid. This generalizes Eymard's theorem and answers a question of Paterson for étale groupoids.

Mean curvature flow into an ambient Riemannian manifold evolving by Ricci-Bourguignon flow

First: 2026-11-16T18:16:13+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-16T18:16:13+00:00
Comments: 29 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study the mean curvature flow in Riemannian manifolds evolving by the Ricci-Bourguignon flow. Using DeTurck's trick, we establish short-time existence and uniqueness for the mean curvature flow in this time-dependent ambient geometry. In the case where the ambient metric evolves by a self-similar solution to the Ricci-Bourguignon flow, we characterize the associated mean curvature solitons and derive a Huisken monotonicity-type formula adapted to the Ricci-Bourguignon setting. As a consequence, we show that, in compact ambient manifolds, type-I singularities of the mean curvature flow in a gradient shrinking Ricci-Bourguignon soliton background have, after suitable parabolic rescaling, weighted minimal hypersurfaces as their limiting models, while in the noncompact setting, we establish the corresponding result for a gradient shrinking Schouten soliton background. This provides a geometric framework for understanding how the ambient scalar curvature influences the formation and structure of singularity models for the mean curvature flow. We also show how to construct explicit examples of mean curvature flow in a gradient Ricci-Bourguignon soliton background.

Abstract
We study $ \mathrm{U}(1) $-invariant Cayley submanifolds in $ \mathbb{R}^{8} $, under the action with weights $ (e^{i θ_{1}}, e^{i θ_{2}}, e^{-i(θ_{1} + θ_{2})}, 1) $ on coordinates $ (z_{1}, z_{2}, z_{3}, z_{4}) \in \mathbb{C}^{4} $. Spectral synthesis with the complexity parameter in $\mathbb{Z}_N^d$
Authors: Alex Iosevich, Zhihe Li, Ke Yu
First: 2026-09-21T20:53:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-16T18:04:17+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study asymptotic spectral synthesis on $\mathbb Z_N^d$ using the Fourier ratio, the quotient of the counting-measure $\ell^1$ and $\ell^2$ norms of the unitary Fourier transform. For supports of cardinality $N^{α+o(1)}$, the Fourier-ratio concentration exponent satisfies $0\leqκ\leq\fracα{2}$. We prove a finite-scale synthesis estimate that uses either the Fourier ratio of a function or that of its support indicator. In particular, the indicator condition applies uniformly to arbitrary weights on the support. Uniform $\ell^p$ bounds imply decay of the largest Fourier coefficient when $2\leq p<\frac{2(d-2κ)}{α-2κ}$, with every finite $p\geq2$ allowed at maximal concentration. Random unions of cosets show that the finite critical exponent is sharp for every admissible pair in each prescribed ambient dimension, along prime-power moduli. We also determine the exact synthesis constant for sets with a transitive group of affine symmetries. For paraboloids this constant is an explicit divisor sum. The formula proves endpoint synthesis along powers of a fixed odd prime, although the endpoint fails along primes. Along products of distinct small primes, synthesis extends to a larger exponent. These families have the same Fourier-ratio exponent. Finally, we relate Fourier concentration to generalized Salem estimates, additive energy, Fourier algebra norms, and necessary conditions for extension estimates.

Abstract
We construct a compact operator $T$ with trace class self-commutator and vanishing Helton-Howe measure such that $T\oplus S-M\notin \mathcal{S}_2$ for every bounded operator $S$ and every bounded normal operator $M$. This gives counterexamples to Voiculescu's normal dilation and stable classification conjectures. The construction uses magnetic shifts on finite square tori. Their real parts have commutators of uniformly bounded trace norm, while their frame errors admit logarithmic lower bounds in the lattice size. A summability criterion for frame error turns these finite-dimensional estimates into a compact counterexample. The same operator inequalities yield a rank profile invariant under Hilbert-Schmidt perturbations and normal stabilization, which distinguishes the example from zero.

Abstract
We consider a general bond percolation on an infinite locally finite tree, where the edge retention probabilities $p_e$ are replaced by $\min\{1,q_{|e|}p_e\}$, where $\{q_n\}_{n\ge 1}$ is a sequence of positive perturbation factors and $|e|$ denotes the distance between the edge $e$ and the root. Abstract
The conjecture of Bando, Kasue, and Nakajima asserts that every simply connected Ricci-flat $4$-manifold that is asymptotically locally Euclidean (ALE), that is, asymptotic at infinity to a flat cone $\mathbb{R}^4/Γ$, is hyperkähler for one of its orientations. A diagonal solution $h = (h_1, \dots, h_r)$ to the Hitchin equation on a cyclic Higgs bundle yields $r-1$ Hermitian metrics $H_1, \dots, H_{r-1}$ on $T_X\rightarrow X$, defined as $H_j \coloneqq h_j^{-1} \otimes h_{j+1}$ for each $j=1,\dots, r-1$, while $h_1$, $h_r$, and $q$ yield a Hermitian metric $H_r$ on $T_X \rightarrow X$ which is degenerate at the zero set of $q$. A diagonal solution is said to be complete if the Kähler metrics induced by $H_1,\dots, H_{r-1}$ are all complete. Li--Mochizuki established a theorem stating that, for any Riemann surface $X$ and any $q$ that is non-zero unless $X$ is hyperbolic, there exists a unique complete solution $h$ to the Hitchin equation for the cyclic Higgs bundle $(\K_r,Φ(q))$ with $\det(h)=1$. The holomorphic section $q$ induces a subharmonic weight function $φ_q=\frac{1}{r}\log|q|^2$ on $K_X\rightarrow X$, and the Hitchin equation depends on $q$ only through this weight function. In previous work, the author introduced a variant of the Hitchin equation associated with an arbitrary subharmonic weight function $\varphi$. In this paper, we extend the Li--Mochizuki theorem to complete solutions of the extended Hitchin equation associated to any subharmonic weight function $\varphi$ that is not identically $-\infty$ unless $X$ is hyperbolic.

Abstract
It is well-known that one can recover a function $f$ bandlimited to $[-δ,δ]$ ($0<δ<π$) from its finite oversampling data $f(j),-n+1\le j\le n$, with an approximation error exponentially decaying to zero as $n$ tends to infinity. Various regularized Shannon sampling formulas have been proposed to achieve such exponential approximation. A recent sinh regularized formula achieved so far the best rate $$ O(\frac1{\sqrt{n}}\exp(-(π-δ)n)). $$ We establish that the optimal recovery algorithm in the worst-case scenario achieves an approximation error $ρ_{n,δ}$ satisfying $$ \frac{c_*}{\sqrt{n}}(\tan\fracδ4)^{2n+1/2}\le ρ_{n,δ}\le \frac{C_*}{\sqrt{n}}(\tan\fracδ4)^{2n+1/2}, 0<δ\leqπ, n\geq1, $$ where $c_*,C_*$ are explicit positive constants independent of $n$ and $δ$. This result identifies the best possible exponential rate to reconstruct bandlimited functions from two-sided finite oversampling data. In other words, $$ ρ_{n,δ} \asymp \frac{\sqrt{\tan\fracδ4}}{\sqrt{n}}\exp(-c_δ(π-δ)n), $$ where $c_δ$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,π]$ and $$ \lim_{δ\toπ^-}c_δ=1,\ \lim_{δ\to 0^+}c_δ=+\infty. $$ For one-sided sample data $f(j)$, $-n+1\le j\le 0$, we also obtain the sharp approximation order of optimally recovering function values on $[0,1]$: $$ ρ_{n,δ}^-\asymp \sqrt{δ/π}\sin^n(δ/2),\ 0<δ<π,\ n\ge1, $$ which improves the celebrated result of H. J. Landau on extrapolation of bandlimited functions.

Authors: Michael Beeson

Abstract
An $N$-tiling of triangle $ABC$ by triangle $T$ (the ``tile'') is a way of writing $ABC$ as a union of $N$ copies of $T$ overlapping only at their boundaries. Let the tile $T$ have angles $(α,β,γ)$, and sides $(a,b,c)$. This paper takes up the case when $3α+ 2β= π$. Then there are (as was already known) exactly five possible shapes of $ABC$ not similar to $T$: either $ABC$ is isosceles with base angles $α$, $β$, or $α+β$, or the angles of $ABC$ are $(2α,β,α+β)$, or the angles of $ABC$ are $(2α, α, 2β)$. We prove that the tile is rational (the ratios of its sides are rational). We then exhibit several families of previously unknown tilings. We also exhibit, for each shape, a Diophantine equation in $N$ and the parameters of the tile that must have a solution whenever there is a tiling of some triangle of that shape. By means of these Diophantine equations, some conclusions about the possible values of $N$ are drawn. We prove, for example, that there is no $N$-tiling with $N$ prime when $3α+ 2β= π$, for three of the five shapes; that question remains open for the shape $(2α,β,α+β)$ and for isosceles triangles with base angles $β$. These equations also imply that for each $N$, there is a finite set of possibilities for the tile $(a,b,c)$ and the triangle $ABC$. (Usually, but not always, there is just one possible tile.) For each of the five shapes, we give a construction method that works under certain sufficient conditions, but there remain values of $N$ that satisfy our necessary conditions and for which we do not know whether a tiling exists. All results have been formalized in Lean.
