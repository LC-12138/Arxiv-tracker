Matching Random Point Sets with Minimum Distortion
Authors: Itai Benjamini, Mark Braverman, Elad Tzalik, Georgii Zakharov
First: 2026-11-04T18:02:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-04T18:02:57+00:00
Comments: 47 pages, 9 figures. Includes a six-page technical overview explaining the main ideas and proof techniques
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
How well can two independent random samples from a metric measure space be matched while preserving pairwise distances? Concretely, given two independent copies, each of $n$ points sampled from the space, what is their typical metric distortion as finite $n$-point metric spaces? We prove that the space $[0,1]$ equipped with the uniform measure already exhibits a rich behavior. We show that with high probability the distortion of the two independent samples is $n^{2/3}$ up to polylogarithmic factors. The lower bound reflects two competing obstructions. A bijection that maps points close to their original positions or their reflections in $[0,1]$ must expand the smallest gaps, in both directions. On the other hand, we show that large displacement from both the identity and reflection itself forces large distortion. Balancing these obstructions yields the lower-bound exponent $2/3$. For the upper bound, we construct a partial matching of the points that maps each gap between consecutive points of length $d\lesssim n^{-4/3}$ to a pair at distance $\widetilde{O}(dn^{1/3})$, with analogous control in the reverse direction. We then use a delicate multiscale oscillation construction to extend this partial matching to a low-distortion bijection.
Summary / 总结
How well can two independent random samples from a metric measure space be matched while preserving pairwise distances?

Abstract
We investigate the spectral and boundedness properties of the inverse Ornstein--Uhlenbeck operator $\mathcal{A}^{Q,B}$ and its associated semigroup in $L^p$ spaces with respect to the inverse Gaussian measure. Abstract
We give a complete characterization of the kernels $Ω\in \Lup^1(\mathbb S^{d-1})$ for which the rough singular integral $\T_Ω$ and its associated maximal operator $\M_Ω$ satisfy pointwise bounds in terms of $\Int_1(|\nabla \cdot|)$ and $\M_1(|\nabla \cdot|)$, respectively, where $\Int_1$ is the Riesz potential and $\M_1$ is the fractional maximal operator. In the Lebesgue scale, we show that, for $d\geq 3$, every rough operator associated to a mean-zero kernel $Ω\in\Lup^{d-1,\infty}(\mathbb S^{d-1})$ satisfies these pointwise bounds, and that the exponent $d-1$ is sharp. In dimension $d=2$, we use complex-analytic methods to establish a new pointwise bound in terms of the complex derivatives $\bar\partial f$ and $\partial f$, which are valid for every mean-zero $Ω\in\Lup^1(\mathbb S^1)$. As a consequence, we obtain new Sobolev bounds for rough singular integrals in the plane.

Abstract
A classical theorem of Micallef says that if $F \colon (Σ, g) \to \mathbb{R}^4$ is a stable minimal immersion of an oriented $2$-dimensional complete Riemannian manifold (that is parabolic) into $\mathbb{R}^4$, it is necessarily holomorphic with respect to some parallel orthogonal complex structure on $\mathbb{R}^4$. This is a necessary assumption for such a theorem to hold (as it is a consequence of the conclusion), and it is automatically satisfied in the classical case $k=1$. We also briefly discuss possible further generalizations of such a result to other calibrations and to Smith maps.

Abstract
We study when the number of spinors satisfying the same generalised Killing equation for a symmetric endomorphism field $W$ forces local realisation as a Euclidean hypersurface. Let $N=2^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}$ be the rank of the complex spinor bundle. We show that in dimension $n\geq4$, if the complex dimension of the solution space exceeds $N/2$, then the metric and $W$ satisfy the Gauss--Codazzi equations. This is proved in the smooth category by combining the curvature defects into a single bivector and bounding the dimension of its kernel on spinors, using algebraic curvature symmetries to handle exceptional cases. Examples obtained from non-flat four-dimensional hyperkähler manifolds show that this threshold is sharp for every $n\geq4$. In dimension three, the Heisenberg group carries the maximal number of common solutions while failing the Euclidean Gauss equation. Further, in the real analytic category, we use existing extension theorems for Einstein metrics and parallel spinors to extend all common solutions to parallel spinors on a single local Ricci-flat ambient manifold. Using Wang's counts of parallel spinors, we obtain an independent proof of the sharp threshold for real analytic data.

Abstract
Let $V_{g,n}$ be the Weil--Petersson volume of the moduli space of hyperbolic surfaces of genus $g$ with $n$ cusps. The fixed-$n$ large-genus expansion of Mirzakhani and Zograf has correction terms polynomial in $n$ of degree $2i$ at order $g^{-i}$, and is no longer asymptotically ordered by powers of $1/g$ when $n$ is of order $\sqrt g$. We prove an asymptotic expansion in powers of $1/g$, uniform in $n\ge0$. For $n\ge1$, its coefficients are analytic in a density parameter on a neighborhood of a compact interval, including the limits $n/g\to0$ and $n/g\to\infty$. Its explicit leading term contains no undetermined constant and has relative error $O(1/g)$; truncation at a sufficiently small multiple of $\sqrt g$ gives relative error $O(\mathrm{e}^{-a\sqrt g})$ for some $a>0$. For fixed $n$ this confirms Zograf's conjecture that the constant in the Mirzakhani--Zograf asymptotics is $1/\sqrtπ$. We also determine, at every cusp density, the asymptotic profiles of the volume ratios $V_{g,n}/V_{g-1,n+2}$ and $V_{g,n+1}/(2gV_{g,n})$ and, in all degrees, the asymptotic ratios of adjacent coefficients of the one-boundary volume polynomial; this answers two questions of Mirzakhani. The proof deforms mixed $κ_1$--$ψ$ intersection numbers into pure $ψ$ intersection numbers along a family whose recursion has nonnegative coefficients, determines the overall constant through Painlevé I without assuming that the Weil--Petersson genus expansion is resurgent, and recovers individual volumes by Gaussian and Poisson coefficient extraction. As an application, the spectral gap of a Weil--Petersson random surface with $n$ cusps tends to zero in probability whenever $n/\sqrt g\to\infty$; this removes the restriction $n/g\to0$ from a theorem of Shen and Wu.

Abstract

Abstract
For every integer $N\ge4$, we construct a fixed smooth, non-locally-conformally-flat metric on the $n$-dimensional unit sphere $\mathbb S^n$ for which the constant $Q_{2N}$-curvature equation admits an $L^\infty$-unbounded sequence of positive solutions. The construction applies for $n\ge2N+20$ when $N=4,5$, $n\ge2N+19$ when $6\le N\le8$, $n\ge2N+18$ when $9\le N\le17$, and $n\ge2N+17$ when $N\ge18$. For each $L \in \mathbb{N} \cup \{0\}$, the metric can be chosen arbitrarily close to the round metric in the $C^L$ norm. It can also be chosen to have a coercive GJMS operator, a strictly positive Green function, and positive Green function mass wherever the Weyl tensor and its covariant derivatives through order $\lfloor(n-2N-4)/2\rfloor$ vanish. Together with the known cases $N=1,2,3$, this establishes noncompactness at every order and proves the sharpness of the companion compactness ranges under the same operator and mass hypotheses. The construction relies on our formula for the fixed-volume Hessian of total $Q_{2N}$-curvature in transverse-traceless (TT) directions at closed Einstein metrics with $\mathrm{Ric}=(n-1)g$. For every $N \in \mathbb{N}$ and $n>2N$, this Hessian is represented by a degree-$N$ polynomial in the Lichnerowicz Laplacian, extending Matsumoto's formula for $n=2N$. For the metric perturbations generated by a fixed algebraic Weyl tensor, the quadratic reduced energy with fixed bubble center is a positive multiple of this Hessian restricted to a four-dimensional TT space. Differentiation in the bubble scale yields a finite matrix whose exact sign analysis establishes the required indefiniteness in the stated dimension ranges. After continuation to real $n$, this matrix changes from negative definite to indefinite at $n_N=2N+a_*+c_*N^{-1}+O(N^{-2})$ as $N\to\infty$, with $a_*\approx16.2018$ and $c_*\approx13.5128$, explaining the eventual bound $n\ge2N+17$.

In this paper, we prove several results concerning the function theory of complete Hermitian manifolds with vanishing Chern curvature, which we refer to as complete Chern-flat manifolds. We also construct examples of complete Chern-flat manifolds with torsion of exponential growth, but the underlying complex manifolds do not admit any complex Lie group structures. Next, we obtain sharp upper bounds on the dimensions of holomorphic functions of polynomial growth on complete Chern-flat manifolds, without any growth assumption on the torsion. Finally, we prove that any complete Chern-flat manifold admitting polynomial-growth holomorphic functions that form a local holomorphic coordinate system at some point is biholomorphic to complex Euclidean space. These results generalize our previous work, where we assume that the torsion has sublinear growth. Our approach combines Cauchy estimates with nilpotent analysis along the orbits of holomorphic flows generated by parallel unitary frames on such manifolds.

Abstract
We determine the universal constant in the large-genus asymptotic expansion of super Weil-Petersson volumes. The argument adopts the uniform-transport strategy for shifted theta intersection numbers. Abstract
Let $Ω\subset\mathbb C$ be a bounded John domain and set $δ(z)=\text{dist}(z,\partialΩ)$. Abstract
We develop a geometric method for studying $C^{2N}$-compactness of positive solutions to the constant $Q_{2N}$-curvature equation on closed Riemannian $n$-manifolds, for every integer $N\ge1$ and every dimension $n>2N$. Assuming the GJMS operator is invertible, its Green function is positive, and the positive mass condition holds, we establish sharp dimension ranges for the compactness of the set of all positive solutions with prescribed positive constant $Q_{2N}$-curvature. The largest dimension for compactness is $24$ for $N=1$ ([21]), $2N+20$ for $N=2,3$ ([11]), $2N+19$ for $N=4,5$, $2N+18$ for $N=6,7,8$, $2N+17$ for $9\le N\le17$, and $2N+16$ for all $N\ge18$. The constructions in [4,5,33,11] for $N=1,2,3$ and in the companion paper [12] for $N\ge4$ give noncompact examples in every dimension exceeding the corresponding compactness bound. We show that these $L^{\infty}$-unbounded examples can be chosen to satisfy the same operator and mass hypotheses, proving sharpness within this class. Our method relates the Pohozaev quadratic forms to the Hessian $D^2\mathscr{F}(g_c)$, where $\mathscr{F}$ is the volume-normalized total $Q_{2N}$-curvature functional and $g_c$ is the standard round metric on $\mathbb{S}^n$. The ambient metric argument of [12] supplies an explicit formula for $D^2\mathscr{F}(g_c)$ on transverse-traceless tensors. The York decomposition projects metric perturbations onto this space. The scalar--vector--tensor decomposition represents the Pohozaev quadratic forms by explicit finite-dimensional matrices. We prove that these matrices are positive definite throughout the stated dimension range, a crucial step in establishing compactness.

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate Ricci solitons in the para-Kenmotsu setting with respect to a $\varphi$-related metallic semi-Riemannian metric arising from the underlying almost paracontact metric structure, under the assumption that the potential vector field is pointwise collinear with the Reeb vector field. We first establish the relation $\tS=S+2nβ\bigl(g-η\otimesη\bigr)$ between the Ricci tensors of the original metric $g$ and the $\varphi$-related metallic metric $\tg$. We then show that the Ricci soliton equation implies that the original metric $g$ is of $η$-Einstein type. By combining this condition with the contracted Bianchi identity, we obtain a rigidity result implying $f=0$, $λ=\frac{2n}{C}$, $S=-2ng$, and consequently $\tS=-\frac{2n}{C}\tg$. Thus, every Ricci soliton in this setting whose potential vector field is pointwise collinear with the Reeb vector field is necessarily trivial, and both $g$ and $\tg$ are Einstein. Finally, we provide two five-dimensional para-Kenmotsu examples: one realizing the resulting trivial Einstein Ricci soliton and another showing that failure of the required Einstein condition prevents the existence of such a soliton.

Abstract
Let $(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathrm{GH}})$ denote the Gromov-Hausdorff space. We prove that every nonempty compact metric space is isometrically embeddable into $(\mathcal{M},d_{\mathrm{GH}})$, answering a question of Iliadis. More precisely, let $D>0$ and let $K$ be a nonempty compact metric space with diameter at most $D$. We construct nonisomorphic separable real Banach spaces whose multiplicative Lipschitz distance is one.
