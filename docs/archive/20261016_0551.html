The $\mathrm{U}$-Derivative Associated with Linear Recurrence Sequences
Authors: Ronald Orozco López
First: 2026-10-14T17:59:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:59:06+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We associate a canonical operator to every linear recurrence sequence admitting a distinct-root Binet representation \(\mathrm{U}_n=\sum_{j=1}^{k}A_jλ_j^n.\) The construction is based on the spectral Binet operator \(\mathcal{B}_\mathrm{U}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}A_jT_{λ_j}, \) where \(T_{λ_j}f(x)=f(λ_jx)\) is the dilation operator associated with the characteristic root \(λ_j\). The corresponding recurrence derivative is defined by \(\mathrm{D}_\mathrm{U}=x^{-1}\mathcal{B}_\mathrm{U}.\) Its fundamental property is the monomial identity \(\mathrm{D}_\mathrm{U}(x^n)=\mathrm{U}_nx^{n-1},\) which establishes a direct correspondence between recurrence sequences and degree-lowering operators. We develop the operator theory of \(\mathrm{D}_\mathrm{U}\), including iteration, a spectral product rule, the associated Leibniz defect, and a quotient rule. The construction reveals a natural bifurcation determined by the value \(\mathrm{U}_0\), producing the Ward and non-Ward regimes as intrinsic operatorial phenomena. The product structure is shown to be governed by \(k-2\) residual spectral channels, leading to notions of spectral cancellation and spectral balance. As an application, we obtain a complete qualitative classification of second-order recurrence calculi. Using Horadam sequences as a universal second-order model, we prove that every such calculus is determined by the invariant triple \((\mathrm{W}_0,Δ, Z_\mathrm{W}),\) where \(\mathrm{W}_0\) identifies the Ward/non-Ward type, \(Δ\) determines the spectral geometry, and \(Z_\mathrm{W}\) determines regularity or singularity.

Complex generalized spin-c Killing spinors in low dimensions
Authors: José Luis Carmona Jiménez, Alejandro Gil-García, C. S. Shahbazi
First: 2026-10-14T17:52:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:52:44+00:00
Comments: 30 pages. Comments are welcome!
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We classify all complex generalized spin-c Killing spinors with complex Killing function $iμ-α$ on complete Riemannian surfaces, presenting an explicit list of all possible cases. We show that, contrary to previous statements in the literature, there exist complex generalized spin-c Killing spinors on Riemannian surfaces for which $αμ\not\equiv0$. We illustrate the classification by providing several explicit local, compact, and complete examples, including a complete example for which $αμ>0$ everywhere. We then move to three dimensions, proving that a spin-c Riemannian three-manifold admits a complex generalized spin-c Killing spinor with complex Killing function $iμ-α$ if and only if it is a $μ$-exact trans-Sasakian manifold of type $(α,μ)$. In addition, we construct a geodesically incomplete Riemannian three-manifold equipped with a complex generalized spin-c Killing spinor for which neither $μ$ nor $α$ vanishes identically, and then prove that this is not possible if the characteristic vector field of the associated trans-Sasakian structure is complete. These results settle a conjecture by Große and Nakad (Results in Mathematics, 2015) stating that on two- and three-dimensional Riemannian manifolds the complex Killing function of any complex generalized spin-c Killing spinor must be either globally real or globally imaginary.

On the atomic decomposition of complete intersection in flag varieties
Authors: Boris Alexeev, Leonardo F. Cavenaghi, Giovane Galindo, Bogdan Georgiev, Ludmil Katzarkov, Pedro Antonio Muniz Martins
First: 2026-09-18T15:37:09+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:51:17+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We develop a root-theoretic localization formalism for genus-zero Gromov--Witten invariants of flag varieties and of smooth zero loci of globally generated homogeneous vector bundles. The resulting invariants are expressed as finite sums over decorated trees whose contributions are determined by the root system of the ambient flag variety and by the torus weights of the defining bundle. We connect these computations with the Theory of Hodge Atoms of Katzarkov--Kontsevich--Pantev--Yu via the matrix of small quantum multiplication by first Chern class. Let $X$ be a Fano fourfold with $h^{3,1}(X)=1$ arising as a hyperplane section of a suitable Fano fivefold. Its cohomology decomposes into a monodromy-fixed part and the middle vanishing cohomology. This decomposition is preserved by quantum multiplication by the first Chern class. Moreover, it acts by a scalar on the vanishing summand. We show, for the monodromy-fixed block, that (a) if every eigenvalue has algebraic multiplicity at most two and $X$ is Hodge general, then $X$ is irrational; (b) if every eigenvalue has Jordan defect at most one and $X$ is rational, then every weak factorization contains a smooth surface center whose minimal model is a projective K3 surface. Many applications are presented. Using the recently introduced quantum connection approach to irrationality, we obtain from the computations here presented the irrationality of the very general smooth complete intersection of a hyperplane and a quadric in $\operatorname{LG}(3,6)$; the very general smooth quadric section in $G_2/P_2$; and the very general smooth complete intersection of five hyperplanes and a quadric in the spinor 10-fold.

We develop a root-theoretic localization formalism for genus-zero Gromov--Witten invariants of flag varieties and of smooth zero loci of globally generated homogeneous vector bundles.

Abstract
Motivated by a log-convexity question of Bennett and Tao, we consider Gowers-type functionals defined by $L^p$ norms of multiple autocorrelations. We prove sharp bounds in terms of $L^q$ norms and characterise the near-extremisers on Euclidean spaces as well as locally compact abelian groups. We also establish two families of degree-lowering inequalities and study their near-extremisers. Finally, we characterise the $p$ for which the Gowers-type functionals necessarily satisfy the triangle inequality on nonnegative measurable functions.

Proof of the Penrose Conjecture for Maximally Parameterized Initial Data Sets

First: 2026-10-14T17:46:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:46:46+00:00

Abstract
We prove the spacetime Penrose inequality for asymptotically flat initial data sets $(M^n,g,k)$ in all dimensions $n\geq 3$, under the dominant energy condition, for data admitting the 'maximally parameterized form' defined in equation (4.7). In this case, the second fundamental form $k$ is locally parameterized by $n(n+1)/2$ functions, giving the same formal local functional degrees of freedom as an arbitrary symmetric $2$-tensor. The proof is based on a new 1-parameter family of generalized Schoen-Yau identities and their gauge-invariant specialization, inspired by the generalized Jang framework of Bray-Khuri [8, 9]. For maximally parameterized data, these identities produce a Riemannian comparison metric \[ \bar g=g+β\otimesβ\] with nonnegative scalar curvature $R_{\bar{g}} \geq 0$. Under a natural nondegeneracy condition (5.7) at the apparent horizon, the comparison metric admits a smooth minimal completion up to $\partial M^n$. Together with suitable asymptotic control, this reduces the Penrose inequality to its Riemannian counterpart. The all-dimensional conclusion uses the Riemannian Penrose theorem of Bi-Zhu [6], together with a minimizing enclosure argument that allows singular boundaries. More generally, in the spirit of [8, 9], we note that the Penrose conjecture follows whenever a certain system of PDEs (3.18) involving $k$ can be solved, in which case our new identity (3.15) immediately implies $R_{\bar{g}} \geq 0$. A natural problem is thus to regularize our degenerate system to prove a general existence theory, thereby proving the Penrose conjecture. Essentially, we believe this is what the OpenAI paper [27] posted last week on October 6, 2026 claims to have done.

Inverse limits of locally convex Lie groupoids
Authors: Ahmed Gamal Shaltut
First: 2026-10-08T16:48:01+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:36:53+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study strict inverse systems of locally convex Lie groupoids and Lie algebroids. We show that strict inverse limits of Lie algebroids and Lie groupoids inherit natural Lie structures and establish a natural isomorphism of functors $\varprojlim\circ\operatorname{Lie}\cong\operatorname{Lie}\circ\varprojlim$. We then consider inverse systems of integrable Banach-Lie algebroids and their source-simply connected levelwise integrations. We give conditions under which these integrations form a strict inverse system of Lie groupoids and hence their inverse limit integrates the inverse-limit algebroid. Under additional hypotheses on the source-fiber inverse sequence, we describe the obstruction to source-simply connectedness of the resulting integration in terms of the derived inverse limit $\varprojlim^{1}π_2$ of the second homotopy groups of the source fibers. We illustrate the theory with several examples: inverse limits of jet groupoids, current groupoids, and gauge groupoids, including the corresponding inverse-limit Atiyah algebroids and a source-simply connected inverse-limit integration arising from the quaternionic Hopf bundle.

The quasi-convex Rockafellar theorem

First: 2026-10-14T17:36:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T17:36:17+00:00
Comments: 32 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Rockafellar showed that every cyclically monotone operator is contained in the subdifferential of a convex function, and characterized the maximal ones. We establish the quasi-convex analogue of this theorem, with no assumption on the operator: in any separable Banach space, a setting we show to be sharp, every cyclically quasi-monotone operator is contained in the normal cone operator of a quasi-convex function. In finite dimension, we characterize maximal cyclically quasi-monotone operators and show that they are maximal quasi-monotone. Unlike in the convex case, the potential is not unique, and we build it from a preference relation obtained through successive preorders. As applications, we extend Afriat's theorem on revealed preferences in economics to infinite data sets, and obtain Kantorovich-type potentials for a supremal optimal transport problem.

Abstract
We prove a new extension principle for the spherically symmetric Einstein--Maxwell--charged scalar field system. Rather than imposing conditions for mass ratio $2m/r$ as in Christodoulou's small-mass-ratio criterion or as in An--Chen--Singh's proof [1] with $2m/r<3/8$, here we work through a proposal by An-Tan [2] with finite Doppler exponent (blueshift). As we were about to release our preprint, Cicortas communicated to us that together with his collaborators, they concomitantly used a different approach to obtain the corresponding extension principle with finite blueshift (Doppler exponent) for the uncharged Einstein-scalar field system. Hence, we decided to put the current paper as an appendix to An-Tan [2], thereby completing the proof of the weak cosmic censorship for the Einstein-Maxwell-charged scalar field system. At the same time, we are working with Cicortas and his collaborators to write a new preprint containing both approaches for the Einstein-scalar field system.

A Scale-Critical $μ<\frac38$ Extension Principle for Spherically Symmetric Einstein-Scalar Field System

Comments: 31 pages; obtained in spring 2025

Abstract
We present a new quantitative, scale-critical $C^1$ extension principle for the spherically symmetric Einstein--scalar field system. We derive estimates for solutions in the causal past of a possibly singular endpoint $\mathcal{O}$ of the center. Assume that there is no trapped surface in this region and assume that the Doppler exponent (blue shift) along each incoming null cone is uniformly bounded. We then prove that the solution can extend regularly across $\mathcal{O}$ provided the mass ratio $μ=2m/r$ satisfies $\supμ<\frac38$. This improves the small-mass-ratio condition in Christodoulou's extension principle by an explicit threshold of order one. Our proof has three new ingredients. First, we derive several new uniform scale-critical pointwise estimates for the scalar field and its derivatives. Second, we obtain refined estimates in an exterior self-similar domain within the triangular causal past region. Third, we establish a family of higher-order Morawetz-type identities and identify the ranges of $μ$, with which there are coercive spacetime estimates. The unweighted identity yields the threshold $μ\leq\frac14$, while employing extra self-similar weight raises the threshold to $μ<\frac38$ and provides a weighted control, which implies that the solution's BV norm along an outgoing null cone is small, so that Christodoulou's theory of bounded variation solutions gives the extension.

Given $0\le m<n$ and a positive Radon measure $μ$ on $\mathbb R^n$ with at most polynomial growth, assume that it has finite $m$-dimensional \emph{Gaussian discrepancy}, defined as $$\mathcal D(μ):=\limsup_{R\to\infty}\Bigl[R^2\sup_{x\in\mathrm{spt}μ}|G_R^μ(x)-1|\Bigr],$$ where $G_R^μ(x):=(4πR^2)^{-m/2}\int_{\mathbb R^n} e^{-|z-x|^2/(4R^2)}\,dμ(z)$. Provided that $μ$ admits a flat blow-down $\mathcal H^m\llcorner V$ (an assumption that cannot be dropped entirely), we prove that there exists a finite (smallest) $0\leδ<\infty$, called the \emph{width} of $μ$, such that $$\mathrm{spt}μ\subseteq \bar B_δ(V+x_0),\quad δ^2\le C(m,n)\mathcal{D}(μ),$$ where $\bar B_δ(V+x_0)$ denotes the closed $δ$-neighborhood of a suitable translate of $V$. Note that the exponent is optimal because of scale invariance. In particular, when $\mathcal D(μ)=0$, the support of $μ$ is included in an $m$-dimensional affine plane. We use this fact to give a simpler proof of Preiss's rectifiability theorem: while still based on Gaussian averages of $μ$, this new proof avoids showing uniqueness (and hence conicality) of blow-downs of $m$-uniform measures, as well as studying Gaussian-weighted moments beyond second order. The strategy of proof consists of an anisotropic blow-down of $μ$, followed by an analysis of the resulting Young measure, using a rigidity lemma involving the heat semigroup.

Abstract
We prove an \(L\log L\)-endpoint Cwikel estimate on quantum tori $\mathbb T_θ^d$, i.e., for every \(d\geq 1\) and \(x\in L\log L(\mathbb T_θ^d)\), \begin{equation*} (1-Δ)^{-\frac{d}{4}}λ(x)(1-Δ)^{-\frac{d}{4}}\in\mathcal{L}_{1,\infty} \end{equation*} with a bound depending only on the dimension, where $λ(x)$ is the left multiplication by $x$. The main ingredient is an abstract weak Hilbert--Schmidt estimate for multiplication operators in finite von Neumann algebras whose \(L_2\)-spaces admit orthonormal bases of unitaries. When $θ=0$, our argument also gives a simpler proof of the $L\log L$ Cwikel estimate on classical tori. For $d\geq2$, we use the $C^*$-algebraic principal symbol map to prove Weyl's laws for finite sums \[ \sum_{\ell=1}^N S_\ell(1-Δ)^{-d/4}λ(x_\ell) (1-Δ)^{-d/4}T_\ell, \] where $x_\ell\in L\log L(\mathbb T_θ^d)$ and $S_\ell,T_\ell\inΠ$, the $C^*$-algebra generated by $λ(C(\mathbb T_θ^d))$ and Fourier multipliers with continuous symbols homogeneous of degree zero. We also prove a semiclassical Weyl's law for critical Schrödinger operators with $L\log L$ potentials.

Abstract
In this paper, we prove a gradient estimate for holomorphic functions on complete Hermitian manifolds with nonnegative second Ricci curvature. As an application, we establish several Liouville-type theorems for holomorphic functions and mappings between complete Hermitian manifolds under suitable curvature assumptions. In particular, on a complete Hermitian manifold with nonnegative second Ricci curvature and Chern torsion of linear growth, any bounded holomorphic function must be constant. Moreover, we construct examples to show that both the gradient estimate and this Liouville theorem are sharp in a suitable sense. In the second part of the paper, we study holomorphic functions on simply connected complete Hermitian manifolds with vanishing Chern curvature and torsion norm of sublinear growth. Combining the gradient estimate with methods from sub-Riemannian geometry, we obtain a complete characterization of holomorphic functions of polynomial growth on such manifolds.

Abstract
We establish finite-dimensional von Neumann type inequalities for commuting matrix tuples in noncommutative operator balls. Let $Ω=\mathbb{D}_Q$ be the NC operator ball of a linear pencil $Q$ with linearly independent coefficients. If $Ω(1)$ admits a transitive subgroup of automorphisms with NC lifts and $Ω(1)$ has the bounded $H^\infty$-Gleason property at the origin, then $\|f(T)\|\leq C_{Ω,n}\|f\|_{H^\infty(Ω(1))}$ for every commuting tuple $T\inΩ(n)$, with $C_{Ω,n}=(2ρ_ΩK_Ω)^{n-1}$; here $K_Ω$ is the Gleason constant and $ρ_Ω$ is the sum of the suprema of the coordinate functions on $Ω(1)$. The scalar coordinate bounds persist at every matrix level. The hypotheses hold for products of row and column balls, where $ρ_Ω=d$, yielding an unconditional estimate $(2dK_Ω)^{n-1}$. For a general type I Cartan domain $R_{p,q}$, we obtain only a conditional result: the same bound holds provided the scalar domain has the bounded $H^\infty$-Gleason property at the origin, which we do not establish when $\min(p,q)\geq 2$.
