Collapsed translating solitons with planar wings
Authors: Eddygledson Souza Gama, Francisco Martín, Niels Martin Møller
First: 2026-10-22T17:59:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:59:50+00:00
Comments: 36 pages, 2 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study complete embedded translating solitons in R^3 with finite genus and finite entropy. We prove that such collapsed translators whose wings are all of planar type (in a weak topological sense) must be vertical flat planes. In particular, this implies the non-existence result for gluing and doubling constructions involving planes that was conjectured in (14) and announced in (9) as an application of the authors' techniques, under weaker assumptions than previous rigidity results by Khan and Lynch-Tinaglia in (17,19,20). A key ingredient is a sharp new a priori decay estimate in the exterior problem for the horizontal graphical translator equation, showing that bounded solutions to the nonlinear equation, and their gradients, are controlled by multiples of the Green's function of the linear drift Laplacian, regardless of the size of the given L^\infty data. Combined with a graphical description of the infinite ends and the Neves-Tian flux identity, this yields the main rigidity result. As another application, we obtain a full classification of collapsed embedded finite-genus translators of entropy lambda < 3.

Weak rank rigidity for groups with a navigable path system
Authors: Cornelia Drutu, Davide Spriano, Stefanie Zbinden
First: 2026-05-28T12:18:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:47:27+00:00
Comments: 71 pages, 21 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We show that groups with a mild form of non-positive curvature (a navigable path system) satisfy the weak rank rigidity conjecture: they either have linear divergence or a Morse element. This class includes discrete groups of projective automorphisms of open convex cones, Helly groups (answering a question of Genevois), Coxeter groups, weak Garside groups (in particular Deligne's groups and fundamental groups of Salvetti complexes of oriented matroids), hierarchically hyperbolic groups, and other examples. Along the way, we show that those groups satisfy the Morse local-to-global property, providing a unified proof for the whole class. In the metric setting, the same condition of non-positive curvature allows to provide a local definition (that is, in a sense, optimal) of rank one/Morse geodesics, mirroring the one using parallel Jacobi fields from Riemannian geometry; to deduce linearity of divergence from linearity on a sequence; to obtain new cases in which Morse geodesics are strongly contracting. The main new tool introduced is the generalised contraction space, a hyperbolic space that encodes the negative curvature of a given space.

Abstract
The attention mechanism underpins modern large language models, yet their dynamics remain only partially understood. Recent perspectives interpret attention blocks as interacting particle systems, whose mean-field limits correspond to gradient flows of energy functionals on spaces of probability densities on $\mathbb R^d$ equipped with Wasserstein-type metrics. We extend this viewpoint by deriving accelerated attention blocks from inertial, Nesterov-type damped Hamiltonian dynamics on these density spaces, developing the geometry and differential calculus needed to make the construction rigorous. In particular, we introduce a local-mobility geometry for the Otto-Wasserstein and softmax-Wasserstein settings, and a quotient Stein geometry that resolves the degeneracy of the linear-attention kernel to yield a well-defined metric and differential calculus. We provide rigorous foundations when softmax attention admits a gradient-flow formulation and when its inertial counterpart admits a damped Hamiltonian on explicitly characterized domains. For linear attention, we obtain a finite-dimensional solution theory with sharp conditions for global existence versus finite-time second-moment blow-up. We further prove a quantitative link between continuous moment blow-up and the discrete-layer depth at which token norms cross a prescribed threshold. Finally, motivated by the finite-token geometry, we propose geometric corrections to token and momentum updates. In decoder-only, nanoGPT-style experiments on TinyStories and OpenWebText, these corrections lower validation negative log-likelihood on next-token prediction relative to momentum-only controls.

Abstract
We develop a method for studying centralizers of generic elements in some large Polish groups. We identify a condition which enables us to show that the range of each power map is comeager; a strengthening of this condition allows us to prove that a generic element embeds in a flow (a continuous, one-parameter subgroup). This approach enables us to answer the question that motivated this work : we prove that a generic isometry of the Urysohn space (or the Urysohn sphere) embeds in a flow. We also obtain information on the centralizer of a generic isometry; we show that it contains an infinite-dimensional torus and that torsion elements are dense in it. To illustrate the flexibility of our method, we apply it to two other Polish groups. The first is the group of measure-preserving automorphisms of a standard probability space, and we recover results of King and of de la Rue and de Sam Lazaro. Finally we apply our technique to the group of nonsingular automorphisms of a standard probability space, and we deduce that a generic nonsingular automorphism embeds in a flow, answering a question of Danilenko and Silva.

Convex Bernstein limits and sharp recovery on expanding intervals
Authors: Matthew J. Colbrook, Catherine Drysdale
First: 2026-10-22T16:53:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:53:14+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We determine when approximation by convex polynomials on expanding intervals converges to best approximation on the real line by convex entire functions of exponential type at most $σ>0$. For intervals of radius $(n-γ_n)/σ$, where $0\leγ_n<n$ and $γ_n=o(n)$, one margin sequence gives this limit for every convex function of polynomial growth if and only if $γ_n/[n^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3}]\to\infty$. When this condition fails, a fixed uniformly strictly convex entire function has approximation errors with unbounded limsup. We apply the limit theorem to prove existence of the convex Bernstein constant for $|x|^λ$ for every $λ\ge1$, and identify it with an attained entire-function minimum. We also give a sufficient margin for growth bounded by $\exp(a|x|^α)$, $0<α<1$. For arbitrary subexponential growth on the real line, each target with finite entire-function error admits a suitable margin, whereas no single margin is sufficient for the whole class. Further results concern attainment, right continuity with respect to the exponential type, weighted growth conditions, and the endpoints of fixed-fraction approximation intervals.

Abstract
Let A be a unital *-subalgebra of $M_n(C)$ and let $M = U(n)/U(A)$, with the quotient Finsler metric induced by a unitarily invariant norm on $u(n)$, normalized so that $||iP|| = 1$ for rank-one projections $P$. On Densities in the Erdős-Falconer Distance Problems over the Integers
Authors: Eyvindur Ari Palsson, Jian-An Wang
First: 2026-10-07T17:59:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:40:49+00:00
Comments: 37 pages, 9 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Motivated by the classical Erdős and Falconer distance problems and Magyar's work on distance sets in $\mathbb Z^d$, we study discrete analogues of the Erdős-Falconer unpinned and pinned distance problems over the integers. We ask how the discrete Hausdorff dimension of a set $A\subseteq\mathbb Z^d$ influences the size of its squared-distance sets, measured by generalized upper natural density and upper discrete mass dimension. We propose conjectures for both problems and prove the unpinned conjecture in dimensions $d=1,2,3$ and the pinned conjecture in dimensions $d=1,2$. For the unpinned problem in higher dimensions, we obtain explicit lower bounds for the dimensions of distance sets using elementary integer sphere counting, results on the Erdős distinct distances problem, and $\ell^p$-improving inequalities for discrete spherical averages. For the pinned problem, we obtain lower bounds for the dimensions of distance sets for some or every pin. We also establish more general results involving upper discrete mass dimension, upper discrete Banach dimension, and affine dimension, including bounds on the affine dimension of the exceptional set of pins. We construct examples showing that both conjectures are sharp in low dimensions. Further examples demonstrate how the distinctions among discrete Hausdorff dimension, upper discrete mass dimension, and upper discrete Banach dimension lead to different phenomena in the unpinned and pinned distance problems, including limitations on the nontrivial upper bounds these dimensions can provide for the dimensions of distance sets.

Abstract
We show that there exist exotic spheres with metrics of strictly positive sectional curvature in infinitely many dimensions: in every dimension $n\equiv3\pmod4$ with $n\geq11$ and $n\not\equiv255\pmod{256}$. Previously, every known closed simply connected manifold of positive sectional curvature in a dimension above $24$ was diffeomorphic to a compact rank-one symmetric space. We then show that, for every $b\geq2$ with $b\not\equiv63\pmod{64}$, there is a map $β$ for which the Eells-Kuiper invariant does not vanish, and thus the corresponding sphere is exotic. A computer calculation also gives the dimensions $n\equiv255\pmod{256}$ below $2^{16}$.

Robust Tangent Space Estimation via Laplacian Eigenvector Gradient Orthogonalization
Authors: Dhruv Kohli, Sawyer J. Robertson, Gal Mishne, Alexander Cloninger
First: 2025-10-02T17:59:45+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:28:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Estimating the tangent spaces of a data manifold is a fundamental problem in geometric data analysis. The standard approach, Local Principal Component Analysis (LPCA), struggles in high-noise settings due to a critical trade-off in choosing the neighborhood size. Selecting an optimal size requires prior knowledge of the geometric and noise characteristics of the data that are often unavailable. In this paper, we propose a spectral method, Laplacian Eigenvector Gradient Orthogonalization (LEGO), that utilizes the global structure of the data to guide local tangent space estimation. Instead of relying solely on local neighborhoods, LEGO estimates the tangent space at each data point by orthogonalizing the gradients of low-frequency eigenvectors of the graph Laplacian. We provide two theoretical justifications of our method. First, a differential geometric analysis on the tubular neighborhood of a manifold shows that gradients of the low-frequency Neumann eigenfunctions of the tube align closely with the manifold's tangent bundle, while an eigenfunction with high gradient in directions orthogonal to the manifold lies deeper in the spectrum. Second, a random matrix theoretic analysis also demonstrates that low-frequency eigenvectors are robust to sub-Gaussian noise. These results allow us to derive the asymptotic scaling and stability of the estimated eigenvector gradients. Numerical experiments demonstrate that LEGO yields tangent space estimates that are significantly more robust to noise than those from LPCA, resulting in marked improvements in downstream tasks such as manifold learning, boundary detection, and local intrinsic dimension estimation.

Estimating the tangent spaces of a data manifold is a fundamental problem in geometric data analysis.

Abstract
A central problem in harmonic analysis is to understand how frequency resolution constrains stability under nonlinear changes of coordinates. Motivated by Mallat's wavelet scattering theory, we establish a sharp resolution--robustness principle for nonlinear representations built from two-dimensional wave-packet systems, showing that the transverse geometry of the frequency channels determines the critical Sobolev regularity. Whenever the channels are thinner than in the wavelet regime, arbitrarily small smooth deformations can move high-frequency mass between adjacent channels, producing instability already at the level of the linear filter bank and the first scattering layer. Together, the results provide the first intrinsic deformation-stability theory for non-wavelet Euclidean scattering transforms and identify a microlocal transport mechanism governing robustness across nonlinear multiscale representations.

Abstract
We establish the stability of the optimal non-endpoint Fourier restriction range under smooth flat perturbations of curved-root surfaces in $\mathbb R^3$. First: 2026-10-22T14:51:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T14:51:44+00:00
Comments: 29 pages. All results first posted 7 Oct 2026, https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23214254
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We prove that every bounded truncated Toeplitz operator on a Hardy model space admits a bounded symbol if and only if its defining inner function is one-component, meaning that it has a connected sublevel set at some level in $(0,1)$. This resolves the bounded symbol conjecture of Baranov, Chalendar, Fricain, Mashreghi and Timotin and, through known equivalences, the embedding and weak factorization conjecture of Baranov, Bessonov and Kapustin. A uniform bound $R$ for the ratio of least bounded symbol norm to operator norm on the rank-one family associated with point evaluation in the disk already forces this geometry. The corresponding bound for all bounded truncated Toeplitz operators is at most $CR\log(e+R)$, where $C$ is absolute, and the logarithmic factor is optimal. In a nonzero de Branges space, the same geometric condition characterizes when every sufficiently small equivalent change of norm preserving the de Branges structure is represented exactly by reweighting the original integral with a positive density bounded above and away from zero. This characterization remains valid when the norm changes are restricted to discrete sampling norms. Outside the one-component class, every model space admits self-adjoint trace class truncated Toeplitz operators without bounded symbols, with arbitrarily small norm and singular values satisfying any prescribed positive upper bounds.
Summary / 总结
We prove that every bounded truncated Toeplitz operator on a Hardy model space admits a bounded symbol if and only if its defining inner function is one-component, meaning that it has a connected sublevel set at some level in $(0,1)$.

Abstract
We derive integral representations for the $n$-th derivative with respect to the order of Bessel functions of the first, second and third kinds, as well as the modified Bessel function and the Macdonald function. Based on these integral representations, we have developed a highly-efficient algorithm for the numerical evaluation of these derivatives with respect to the order, applicable to any complex argument and any real order.

Primes are Complete for a Class of Cantor Spectral Pair

Abstract
We study the complete number problem for a class of self-similar spectral measures on the real line. We say that a real number $t$ is complete for a spectral pair $(μ,Λ)$ if $(μ,tΛ)$ is also a spectral pair. In this paper we consider the partial homogeneous Cantor measure $μ_{N^r,\mathcal D}$ and its canonical spectrum. Our main result fully characterizes all prime powers $p^n$ that are complete for $p>N$. In particular, we obtain a necessary and sufficient condition for an arbitrary prime $p$ to be complete. {We further establish a connection between the problem and certain Diophantine equations}, including the Nagell--Ljunggren equation, and obtain complete number results for a class of related models. As applications, known solutions of the Nagell--Ljunggren equation provide complete and incomplete prime-power examples.

We study the complete number problem for a class of self-similar spectral measures on the real line.
Endpoint weighted Fourier estimate with vanishing spherical trace

Abstract
We establish an endpoint weighted Fourier estimate under a vanishing restriction condition on the unit sphere. Let $n \geq 2$ and $p_c = 2(n+1)/(n+3)$. We prove that every $f \in L^{p_c}(\mathbb{R}^n)$ with zero spherical Fourier restriction satisfies $$ \left\| \bigl|1-|ξ|^2\bigr|^{-1/2}\widehat{f}(ξ) \right\|_{L^2(\mathbb{R}^n)} \leq C_n \|f\|_{L^{p_c}(\mathbb{R}^n)}, $$ where the restriction condition is understood through the continuous Tomas--Stein restriction operator. This result gives a negative answer to Goldberg's counterexample-existence question and supplies a missing endpoint.

We establish an endpoint weighted Fourier estimate under a vanishing restriction condition on the unit sphere.

Abstract
The main goal of this work is to introduce derived Lie n-groupoids and their shifted symplectic structures. We further define shifted lagrangian structures and prove that their composition is well defined under suitable conditions. As an application, we show that our framework incorporates several reduction procedures at critical values, including: classical Hamiltonian reduction, group valued moment maps, Poisson Lie group valued moment maps and Mikami-Weinstein for proper symplectic groupoids.

A sharp diameter-mass inequality on Einstein manifolds and ADM mass
Authors: Xuezhang Chen, Jiaxue Gan, Yalong Shi, Nan Wu
First: 2026-10-22T14:26:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T14:26:47+00:00
Comments: 25 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Let $(M^n,g), n\geq 4$ be a close Einstein manifold with positive scalar curvature and $G$ be the Green function of the conformal Laplacian. The ADM mass of the asymptotically flat metric $\hat g:=G^{4/(n-2)} g$ is an important geometric invariant. When either $n=5$ or $n\geq 6$ with Weyl vanishing condition, we prove a mass formula, generalizing Gursky-Malchiodi's formula in dimension $4$. An important step is an extension of Viaclovsky's Green function expansion in higher dimensions. Next we prove a sharp diameter-mass inequality on Einstein manifolds. Finally we present two applications. First, we prove a mass gap theorem for Einstein manifolds. Second, for Collins-Székelyhidi's family of Sasaki-Einstein metrics $g_i$ on $\mathbb{S}^5$ with volume going to zero, the sequence of the masses $m(\hat{g_i})$ blows up uniformly as $i\to\infty$.

Lawlor's curvature criterion and topologically trivial area-minimizing hypercones of Hsiang type

Abstract
For a closed embedded minimal hypersurface in a unit sphere, we show that the existence of a uniform vanishing angle in Lawlor's criterion suffices for its cone to minimize area. The normal-wedge condition follows from Howard's rolling theorem. We derive explicit curvature bounds and apply them to the reported Hsiang-Sterling curvature estimates for topologically trivial cones. The resulting area-minimizing cones give an affirmative answer to the existence question raised by Ilmanen and White (Camb. J. Math., 2015).
