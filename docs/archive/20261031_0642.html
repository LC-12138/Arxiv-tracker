Multicomplexes in Action: Strong Formality of Compact Hyperkähler Manifolds

Abstract
We show that all compact hyperkähler manifolds are bigraded strongly formal in the sense of Milivojević and Stelzig, proving that in the context of pluripotential homotopy theory they play the same role as compact Kähler manifolds in rational homotopy theory. Indeed, the main result arises from general algebraic considerations on categories of objects with more differentials, that of multicomplexes and commutative $n$-differential graded algebras. For them we discuss a standard model category structure, introduce a generalization of the $\partial\bar{\partial}$-Lemma and establish an algebraic formality theorem.

Some remarks on robust features of surfaces in 4-space

Abstract
We study robust features of smooth surfaces in 4-space associated with contact with lines and hyperplanes. We investigate the local and multi-local singularities of orthogonal projections and height functions that describe these contacts and their corresponding geometric loci on the surface. At an $A_4$-singularity of a height function, we show that the $A_3$-curve and the $A_1A_2$-curve are generically tangent with contact of order $2$. We further establish criteria for the versality of the families of height functions and orthogonal projections in terms of the regularity of the $B_2$-curve and $S_2$-curve, respectively. Finally, we obtain the $[A_2]$- and $(A_0S_0)_2$-curves arising from multi-local singularities of orthogonal projections and describe their tangency properties at the $B_3$- and $H_3$-singularities.

Almost Homogeneity Characterizes Almost Bi-Lipschitz Euclidean Embeddability

First: 2026-10-29T17:52:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:52:06+00:00

Abstract
A metric space admits an almost bi-Lipschitz embedding into a Euclidean space if and only if it is almost homogeneous. We prove the sufficient direction, complementing the known necessary condition. More quantitatively, we bound the target dimension and the embedding constants for every logarithmic exponent $γ>1$. The bounds depend only on the covering parameters in the almost homogeneity condition. In particular every doubling metric space admits such an embedding. This answers a question of Margaris and Robinson and the almost bi-Lipschitz part of a question of Olson and Robinson. As a consequence, the difference set of the image is doubling, which answers the difference-set questions of Olson and Robinson and of Margaris.

Debiasing optimal transport: classical and entropic
Authors: Pierre-Cyril Aubin-Frankowski, Virginie Ehrlacher, Gabriele Todeschi
First: 2026-04-21T20:40:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:42:25+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study the notion of debiasability for cost functions arising in optimal transport. We call a symmetric cost function $c:\mathscr{X}\times\mathscr{X}\to R\cup\{+\infty\}$ debiasable if it satisfies $c(x,y)\ge \tfrac{1}{2}c(x,x)+\tfrac{1}{2}c(y,y)$ for all $x,y\in\mathscr{X}$. Building on an equivalent characterization by an inf-representation $c(x,y)=\inf_{z\in\mathscr Z}ψ(x,z)+ψ(y,z)$ for some set $\mathscr Z$ and some function $ψ:\mathscr X\times\mathscr Z\to R\cup\{+\infty\}$, interpreted as a generalization of the midpoint identity for squared geodesic distances, we investigate the debiasability of costs defined on spaces of probability measures. Our primary focus is the entropic regularization of optimal transport across the different regimes of the regularization parameter $\varepsilon\in[0,+\infty]$, encompassing classical optimal transport ($\varepsilon=0$), entropic optimal transport ($\varepsilon>0$), and the Maximum Mean Discrepancy ($\varepsilon=+\infty$). For $\varepsilon\in(0,+\infty)$ we obtain debiasability under negative definiteness of the ground cost, through an explicit Gaussian factorization, and, more generally, under positive semidefiniteness of the kernel $e^{-c/\varepsilon}$ alone, conditions also proved necessary. All our results extend naturally to unbalanced optimal transport settings. Extending our factorizations to include a time parameter $t\in(0,1)$, we construct an entropic interpolation curve given by an intermediate measure $η_t\in\mathscr P(\mathscr Z)$ based on a barycentric formulation, which coincides with the marginals of the Schrödinger bridge between $μ$ and $ν$. As a byproduct, we derive a new proof for the entropic Benamou-Brenier formula under very mild assumptions.

We study the notion of debiasability for cost functions arising in optimal transport.
Willmore inequalities beyond the injectivity radius
Authors: Philippe G. LeFloch
First: 2026-10-29T17:37:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:37:43+00:00
Comments: 20 pages

Abstract
We generalize a method first introduced by Li, Yau, Simon, and Topping (for closed surfaces immersed in the flat Euclidean space), and we establish a global-in-nature estimate, with an explicit cut-locus contribution, on the Willmore energy of closed surfaces that are immersed in a complete Riemannian three-manifold and need not be embedded. Our estimate applies to ambient spaces whose sectional curvature is bounded above and whose injectivity radius may be finite. For positive upper curvature bounds, the global weighted estimates require an explicit radius restriction. The Willmore energy is bounded below by a functional involving the self-intersection number of the surface and the injectivity radius of the ambient space. Furthermore, by investigating in what sense our inequalities are optimal and realized by geodesic spheres in constant curvature spaces, we are led to introduce a weighted version of the Willmore functional, which is adapted to geodesic spheres in the model spaces and takes the sectional curvature of the ambient space into account. As a corollary, we obtain sufficient conditions for an immersed surface in a curved space to be embedded.

Localization in Seven-Dimensional Supergravity
Authors: Florian Gaar, Michèle Levi
First: 2026-10-29T17:25:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:25:00+00:00
Comments: 38 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Seven-dimensional supergravity provides holographic access to strongly coupled six-dimensional superconformal theories through its highest-dimensional supersymmetric AdS vacua. Its unique spinor-bilinear geometry and massive higher-form dynamics make systematic analysis and solution-finding distinctively challenging. Relative equivariant localization can obtain supersymmetric observables in odd dimensions from fixed-point data on an auxiliary even-dimensional filling, without an explicit solution. We extend this approach to seven dimensions in maximal Cartan-gauged supergravity with all gauge fields active. We formulate the Euclidean action and Killing-spinor equations in terms of field strengths, and construct a bilinear system adapted to the change in spinor reality conditions from symplectic-Majorana to Majorana. On the non-null gauged active spinor branch, independent, oppositely R-charged spinors are paired by Majorana conjugation on the same complex background. One algebraic gauging correction and two algebraic bilinear projections suffice to extend the equal-charge sector to independent Cartan charges, and the underlying efficient reorganization also applies in the Lorentzian theory. Massive 3-form self-duality fixes the quartic anomaly class and determines the higher-form fixed-point datum from Abelian moment maps. Localization at the single fixed point of the filling recovers the general supersymmetric AdS$_7$ black-hole action, without extending the solution. We prove gauge-covariant Killing-spinor invariance under the combined supersymmetric Killing flow and R-symmetry action, deriving the linear chemical-potential constraint. Extremizing the constrained entropy functional and requiring real positive stationary entropy yields the nonlinear charge relation within the Euclidean analysis, equivalent to Lorentzian horizon extremality for the known spherical black-hole family.

Seven-dimensional supergravity provides holographic access to strongly coupled six-dimensional superconformal theories through its highest-dimensional supersymmetric AdS vacua.
Differentiation and Shift Property Characterisations of the Group Fourier Transform on the Heisenberg Group

First: 2026-10-29T17:23:25+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:23:25+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recently, Krukowski characterised the classical Fourier transform on the real line in terms of its behaviour with respect to differentiation. For locally compact abelian groups, he obtained a characterisation based on the behaviour of the Fourier transform under the shift. Each of these results assumed that the operator (to be characterised) is an integral transform (with a kernel satisfying suitable regularity conditions), paired with a normalisation which he refers to as the Dirac delta property. In this paper, we obtain analogous results for the group Fourier transform on the Heisenberg group. Here, the differentiation property is formulated in terms of the behaviour of the group Fourier transform with respect to the left-invariant vector fields, and the shift property is based on its behaviour under right translation. Both of these characterise the group Fourier transform on the Heisenberg group, once paired with a Dirac delta property. Our results assume that the transform is continuous and linear, and do not require it to be an integral transform a priori.

Abstract
Standing waves $e^{iωt}u(x)$ of nonlinear dispersive equations, such as the nonlinear Schrödinger and Klein--Gordon equations, have a profile $u$ that solves the semilinear elliptic equation $Δu+f(u)=0$. We prove the existence of radial bound states with a prescribed number of nodal surfaces for this equation on a large class of spherically symmetric manifolds and for a broad class of nonlinearities $f$. Under further assumptions on the metric, we prove uniqueness of such bound states for the model nonlinearity $f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u-u$. Both existence and uniqueness results apply to the hyperbolic spaces $\mathbb{H}^n_λ$ of any curvature $-λ^2$ and dimension $n\geq 3$, and to some manifolds with a singularity at the origin.

Finite-rank rigidity and Nash-curve discreteness for correlation potentials
Authors: Josiah Park
First: 2026-10-29T17:10:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:10:28+00:00
Comments: 22 pages, 0 figures

Abstract
For every nonconstant real polynomial P of degree k and every positive rational exponent a/b, minimizing the spherical energy with potential |P(t)|^{a/b} admits a discrete solution with at most $\binom{d+2ak-1}{2ak}+\binom{d+2ak-2}{2ak-1}$ support points, where a/b is reduced. This finite-replacement consequence needs no denominator threshold. Under a multiplicity-sensitive denominator condition, we prove the stronger conclusion that every minimizer has an explicit uniform support bound; suitable negative positive-semidefinite perturbations preserve that bound. We present these consequences before the machinery. The main arguments simplify irrational-power finite-rank rigidity and give a Nash-curve ramification obstruction, stable under entire scalar additions, for powers, logarithms, and rational poles. The latter yields uniform finite supports on compact semialgebraic curves without assuming that the supports are semialgebraic. The known p-frame finite-replacement principle is distinguished from the extensions developed here. No unconditional discreteness theorem for general irrational p is claimed.

Abstract
Motivated by Zygmund's differentiation conjecture and Stein's conjecture for Lipschitz vector fields, we study bilinear singular integral and maximal operators along variable curves given by $$ \begin{aligned} BH_a^α(f,g)(x) &= \operatorname{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb R} f(x-t) g\left(x + a(x)t^α\right)\,\frac{dt}{t},\\ BM_a^α(f,g)(x) &= \sup_{r>0}\frac{1}{2r}\int_{-r}^{r} \left|f(x-t)g\left(x+a(x)t^α\right)\right|\,dt,\\ BSH_c^α(f,g)(x) &= \operatorname{p.v.} \int_{\mathbb R} f(x-t) g(x + t^α)\, \frac{dt}{t-c(x)}, \end{aligned} $$ where $α> 0$ and $a,c:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ are measurable. For every $α>0$ and $0<A<B<\infty$, we construct a smooth, globally Lipschitz coefficient $a:\mathbb R\to(A,B)$ such that, for every Hölder conjugate pair $1<p,q<\infty$, no strong--type $L^p(\mathbb R)\times L^q(\mathbb R)\to L^1(\mathbb R)$ estimate holds for $BH_a^α$ or $BM_a^α$ on $C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)\times C_c^\infty(\mathbb R)$; moreover, there exist nonnegative smooth inputs in $\bigcap_{s>1}L^s(\mathbb R)$, one of which is compactly supported, whose outputs under $BH_a^α$ and $BM_a^α$ do not belong to $L^1(\mathbb R)$. For any measurable $a:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$, $BM_a^α$ maps $L^p(\mathbb R) \times L^q(\mathbb R)$ boundedly into $L^r(\mathbb R)$ whenever $1<p,q\leq\infty$, $1/r = 1/p + 1/q$, and $r > 1$, and maps $L^p(\mathbb R) \times L^q(\mathbb R)$ into weak $L^1(\mathbb R)$ when $1/p+1/q=1$, with constants independent of $a$. Finally, we investigate the shifted operator $BSH_c^α$, including dyadic-integer-valued $c$, and prove that there is a measurable choice of $c$ such that the operator admits no bounded $L^p(\mathbb R) \times L^q(\mathbb R) \to L^{r,\infty}(\mathbb R)$ estimates in the range $1 \leq p,q \leq \infty$ and $\frac{1}{2}<r<\infty$, resolving an open problem posed by Gaitan and Lie.

Universal Closest Refinement on Measurable Bipartite Relations
Authors: T-H. Hubert Chan
First: 2026-04-11T16:35:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:18:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study the universal closest refinement problem on measurable bipartite relations over standard Borel spaces. Given prescribed side measures, the feasible class consists of finite refinement plans concentrated on the relation and carrying one fixed marginal. The main question is whether this highly nonunique class nevertheless contains a mathematically distinguished class of refinements. We show that the correct one-sided extremal criterion is level-optimal maximin, a levelwise maximin principle formulated through truncation and overflow profiles. We then prove that this structure is exactly the one selected by convex refinement: every minimizer of a strictly convex refinement criterion is level-optimal maximin, while every level-optimal maximin refinement minimizes the full class of relevant proper lower semicontinuous convex divergence functionals. Proportional response then identifies the opposite-side partner and yields a universally closest refinement pair. Our main theorem shows that every such pair is universally closest among all feasible pairs for every divergence satisfying the data-processing inequality under measurable post-processing, and conversely that every universally closest pair has this structure. We also prove a converse paired characterization for strictly convex closest pairs. Finally, we give an equilibrium-theoretic characterization of level-optimal maximin pairs through a naturally associated continuum economy with measure-valued commodities. The measure-theoretic theory requires new tools beyond the finite case, including disintegration, measurable selection, measurable max-flow/min-cut duality, augmentation arguments, and a symmetric density decomposition separating absolutely continuous and singular components.

We study the universal closest refinement problem on measurable bipartite relations over standard Borel spaces.

Abstract
We prove that a two-distance set in $\mathbb R^d$ with distance ratio $1:\sqrt{2}$ has at most $d(d+1)/2$ points for every $d\ge9$. Equality in full affine dimension characterizes the Johnson construction; for $d\ge10$, the full-dimensional condition is automatic. The proof separates configurations according to the connectivity of the short-distance graph. Abstract
In this work we present the following general principle: every positive homogeneous functional on an abstract convex cone satisfying a suitable Brunn-Minkowski inequality gives rise to an information functional -defined as the quotient of the functional by its relative surface area- that satisfies a linear Brunn-Minkowski inequality. We also establish the dual counterpart of this principle for functionals satisfying an analogous convexity property, yielding reverse linear Brunn-Minkowski inequalities for the associated information functionals. Furthermore, we show that homogeneity is necessary within this principle by providing a counterexample to the expected result for the information functional associated to the standard Gaussian measure. As applications, we recover, via an alternative first-order argument, a classical result by Giannopoulos, Hartzoulaki and Paouris (2002) for the ratio of two consecutive quermassintegrals, together with a characterization of the equality case. The same argument extends to the setting of mixed discriminants of symmetric positive definite matrices. Finally, our approach yields new extensions of the Brunn-Minkowski inequality of Dembo, Cover and Thomas, for the classical information functional, both to the $L_p$ setting ($p \geq 1$), where the relative surface area is expressed in terms of Lutwak's first $L_p$ quermassintegral, and to the measure-theoretic framework, for measures with homogeneous densities satisfying some concavity. Moreover, we prove that our principle applies also in the context of the Blaschke addition, both in the volume and in the (mixed) affine surface area cases, obtaining new inequalities as an application of it.

Abstract
Let $V_{g,n}$ be the Weil--Petersson volume of the moduli space of hyperbolic surfaces of genus $g$ with $n$ cusps. The fixed-$n$ large-genus expansion of Mirzakhani and Zograf has correction terms polynomial in $n$ of degree $2i$ at order $g^{-i}$, and ceases to be uniform when $n$ is of order $\sqrt g$. We prove an asymptotic expansion in powers of $1/g$ that holds uniformly in all $n\ge0$, with coefficients analytic in a density parameter that ranges over a compact interval, including the limits $n/g\to0$ and $n/g\to\infty$. Its leading term is explicit, contains no undetermined constant and has relative error $O(1/g)$; truncation at an order proportional to $\sqrt g$ gives relative error $O(\exp(-a\sqrt g))$ for some $a>0$. For fixed $n$ this confirms Zograf's conjecture that the constant in the Mirzakhani--Zograf asymptotics is $1/\sqrtπ$. We also determine, at every cusp density, the limits of the volume ratios $V_{g,n}/V_{g-1,n+2}$ and $V_{g,n+1}/(2gV_{g,n})$ and, in all degrees, the asymptotics of the ratios of adjacent coefficients of the one-boundary volume polynomial; this answers two questions of Mirzakhani. The proof deforms mixed $κ_1$--$ψ$ intersection numbers into pure $ψ$ intersection numbers along a family whose recursion has nonnegative coefficients, fixes the overall constant through Painlevé I without assuming resurgence, and recovers individual volumes by a local limit theorem in the number of cusps. As an application, the spectral gap of a Weil--Petersson random surface with $n\gg\sqrt g$ cusps tends to zero in probability; this removes the restriction $n/g\to0$ from a theorem of Shen and Wu.

Duality and Baire like properties in topological abelian groups
Authors: María V. Ferrer, Salvador Hernández, Isabel Sepúlveda
First: 2026-09-12T17:46:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T16:13:43+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This article investigates the duality of abelian topological groups by delving into the interplay between the algebraic and topological properties of a group $G$ and those of its dual group. In particular, extending the notion of a $g$-barrelled group, we introduce and study the classes of $κ$-barrelled and $ω$-barrelled groups. Our first main result establishes that every $ω$-barrelled group is $\aleph_0$-barrelled (i.e., if every weakly convergent sequence in the dual group is equicontinuous, then every metrizable weakly compact subset of the dual group is also equicontinuous). From this, we deduce that the free topological abelian group $A(K)$ over a metrizable compact space $K$ is determined by the subgroup generated by any of its dense subsets. Furthermore, we prove that if $G$ is a $σ$-compact, $ω$-barrelled group, then its dual group $\widehat{G}_κ$ equipped with the compact-open topology is sequentially complete (complete if $G$ is hemicompact). We also solve an open question posed by Trigos-Arrieta by constructing an explicit example of an $ω$-barrelled metrizable group that fails to be $g$-barrelled. Finally, we investigate the dualization of groups lacking infinite compact subsets, proving that for a totally bounded abelian group $G$, every compact subset of $G$ is finite if and only if its dual group $\widehat{G}$, endowed with the finite-open topology, is unordered Baire-like.

Abstract
We show that if $G$ is a real semi-simple Lie group, and $Γ$ is a discrete subgroup of $G$ containing a subgroup $Σ$ acting ergodically (in a strong sense) on the Furstenberg boundary of $G$, then $Γ$ is not isomorphic to a free product of $Σ$ with $\mathbb{Z}$. Abstract
We present an operator-theoretic framework for the Ollivier--Ricci curvature of (one-parameter families of) random walks generated by positive semigroups on graphs. Abstract
We investigate the structure of kernels of finite-rank perturbations of truncated Toeplitz operators. Abstract
This paper extends the classical results of Eisenhart (1923) and Levi-Civita (1896) on the classification of pairs of germs of Riemannian metrics with the same geodesics, either as parametrized curves (affine equivalence) or as unparametrized curves (projective equivalence), to sub-Riemannian metrics on arbitrary step 2 distributions: at every stable point in the projective case and at every point in the affine case. This extends the results of Zelenko (2004) for contact and even-contact distributions and the affine result of Lin and Zelenko (2024) for step 2 distributions with ad-surjective Tanaka symbols; our projective result covers the ad-surjective case as well. In the affine case, we prove that if a sub-Riemannian metric admits an affinely equivalent metric that is not constantly proportional to it, then locally it is a product of two sub-Riemannian metrics. In the projective case, we prove the 2019 conjecture of Jean, Maslovskaya, and Zelenko on generalized Levi-Civita pairs; in particular, the distribution admits a product structure, the squares of its components are involutive, and the restrictions of the two metrics to each leaf of the corresponding foliation are conformal to each other under the appropriate pullback. The proof is based on a new graph-theoretic formalism, in which compatible moving frames are represented by the vertices and edges of certain graphs. As a byproduct, we introduce a new discrete invariant of step 2 nilpotent graded Lie algebras, the characteristic tuple. The compatible graphs are used on the open dense set where this tuple is locally constant; the resulting bracket relations are identities between smooth tensors, so they extend by continuity across the points where the tuple jumps. Consequently, our classification holds without any assumption of local constancy of the characteristic tuple.

Abstract
This paper is about comparing the Gromov-Hausdorff distance between a pair of sets to the Hausdorff distance between the pair. Recent work on this problem has focused on two settings, Riemannian manifolds and metric graphs. Here, we focus on embedded submanifolds of Euclidean space endowed with the Euclidean metric. We prove that when $\mathcal{M}\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ is a compact connected embedded submanifold without boundary, with positive reach, and $\mathrm{A}\subset\mathcal{M}$ is a subset that is sufficiently dense in $\mathcal{M}$ with respect to the reach, the Gromov-Hausdorff distance between $\mathrm{A}$ and $\mathcal{M}$ is bounded below by $1/2$ of the Hausdorff distance between them. We extend this result in two ways. First, we show that the factor $1/2$ can be improved using Jung's theorem. Second, we prove bounds on the Gromov-Hausdorff distance for pairs of subsets of $\mathcal{M}$. When $\mathbb{S}^{1}$ is equipped with the two-dimensional Euclidean metric, the Gromov-Hausdorff distance between $\mathbb{S}^{1}$ and any sufficiently dense subset of $\mathbb{S}^{1}$ can be expressed explicitly as a function of the Hausdorff distance between the two. We use interleavings between geometric simplicial complexes, nerve lemmas, properties of reach and the nearest-point projection map, and the fundamental class to produce the inequalities for general $\mathcal{M}$. We use the Borsuk-Ulam theorem for $\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{S}^1\subset \mathbb{R}^2$.
