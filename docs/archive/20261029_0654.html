Sharp Stability of Brenier Potentials and its Link to Poincaré Inequalities
Authors: Cyril Letrouit, Tudor Manole
First: 2026-10-27T17:59:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:59:00+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We prove a new functional inequality for uniformly log-concave measures, stronger than the Poincaré inequality. This new inequality bounds the variance of differences of convex functions and is motivated by the stability theory of optimal transport: for a fixed uniformly log-concave source measure, it provides a sharp stability bound for Brenier potentials with respect to variations of the target measure. Applying this inequality to Gaussian measures, we recover the fact that the Bures-Wasserstein distance between two positive semidefinite matrices is equivalent to the Frobenius distance between their square roots. Our proofs hinge upon linearization arguments for the Monge-Ampère equation governing the quadratic optimal transport problem.

Applications of the fixed point theorem for mappings contracting perimeters of triangles: ODEs, PDEs, integer and difference equations
Authors: E. Petrov, R. Salimov, R. Taranets
First: 2026-10-27T17:55:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:55:35+00:00
Comments: 34 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Mappings contracting perimeters of triangles are Banach contractions whenever every point of the underlying metric space is an accumulation point. Consequently, applying the perimeter-contraction theorem on a complete metric space without isolated points yields an existence result already covered by Banach's fixed point theorem. This observation applies, in particular, to operators associated with differential and integral equations acting on standard nontrivial function spaces, such as spaces of continuous functions $C[a,b]$, Lebesgue spaces $L_p$, and Sobolev spaces $W^{k,p}$, equipped with their usual norm metrics. Thus, applications extending beyond Banach's theorem for the same operator and metric require a complete invariant domain containing some number of isolated points. Such a domain may be a closed subset of a standard function space, despite the absence of isolated points in the ambient space. The main goal of this paper is to illustrate applications of the fixed point theorem for mappings contracting perimeters of triangles in settings where the associated operators are not Banach contractions. The differential-equation examples admit interpretations as stylized models combining continuous evolution, nonlocal measurement, and quantized feedback. The integer and difference-equation examples illustrate the effects of rounding on equilibria and periodic responses.

A dichotomy for minimal submanifolds
Authors: Tobias Holck Colding, William P. Minicozzi
First: 2026-07-31T15:05:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:44:10+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We survey a circle of recent results showing that a minimal submanifold obeys a dichotomy: either it fills up space, spreading out like a space-filling curve, or it is confined, and confinement forces quantitative restrictions. On the confined side these restrictions are both geometric and function-theoretic: Euclidean volume growth, an optimal rate of convergence of the density, complex-curve rigidity for stable surfaces in $\bf{R}^4$, and a Liouville theorem forcing slowly growing harmonic functions on minimal disks to be constant. A prototypical restriction is Euclidean volume growth, which in these results is forced by the geometry rather than assumed. The mechanism is a volume doubling theorem that converts geometric confinement into quantitative rigidity: a stationary integral varifold trapped in a thin slab at a given scale cannot double its volume by more than a universal factor. We explain how this principle, and the height-excess bounds behind it, produce Euclidean volume growth for submanifolds of sublinearly growing height in every dimension and codimension, the optimal density rate in a slab, the complex-curve and Liouville rigidity above, a higher-codimension Bernstein theorem for disks, one-sided volume bounds, and an optimal stable Bernstein theorem in all dimensions generalizing Moser, Bombieri-De Giorgi-Miranda, Caffarelli-Nirenberg-Spruck and Ecker-Huisken. We describe three regimes with sharp borderlines. In narrow cones the volume grows polynomially with exponent $n+O(\varepsilon^2)$, and the order $\varepsilon^2$ is attained; in wide cones the growth can be arbitrarily fast. For holomorphic curves in $\mathbb{C}^2$, there are also three regimes and the borderline is determined by the Kähler angle.

Uniform large-genus asymptotics of Weil-Petersson volumes
Authors: Zhiyi Li, Qihang Wang
First: 2026-10-26T14:21:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:03:45+00:00
Comments: 84 pages, no figures. v2: added uniform boundary and tight-spectrum results; expanded proofs and updated references, including removal of a withdrawn dependency. An earlier version was first made public on Zenodo on 16 October 2026: https://zenodo.org/records/23414478
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Let $V_{g,n}$ denote the Weil--Petersson volume of the moduli space of genus-$g$ hyperbolic surfaces with $n$ cusps. Mirzakhani and Zograf obtained a complete asymptotic expansion as $g\to\infty$ for fixed $n$; its terms are effectively powers of $n^2/g$, so it degenerates once $n$ is of order $\sqrt g$. We prove an asymptotic expansion in inverse powers of the genus that is uniform in all $n\ge0$. The leading term is an explicit saddle-point expression whose saddle moves with the cusp density $n/g$. It has relative error $O(g^{-1})$, and truncation at an order proportional to $\sqrt g$ gives relative error $O(\exp(-a\sqrt g))$ for some $a>0$. For fixed $n$, the leading term confirms Zograf's conjecture that the constant in the Mirzakhani--Zograf asymptotics is $1/\sqrtπ$. We also determine the asymptotic profiles of volume ratios, uniformly in the number of cusps, and of adjacent coefficients of the one-boundary volume polynomial, uniformly in the degree; this answers two questions of Mirzakhani. The proof uses a positive recursion for mixed $κ_1$--$ψ$ intersection numbers, normalized at the pure-$ψ$ endpoint, and a local limit theorem in the number of cusps.

Fisher Completions and Statistical Regularization of Wrapped Gaussian Families
Authors: Gunhee Cho, Hyun Chul Jang
First: 2026-10-27T16:47:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:47:48+00:00
Comments: 33 pages, 5 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We determine the intrinsic Fisher completion of the full-covariance wrapped Gaussian family on a fixed torus and relate its geometry to uniform estimation. The completion is homeomorphic to the total-variation closure. Every added law is, in suitable integer torus coordinates, a product of a uniform law and a lower-dimensional wrapped Gaussian. All these densities are positive and real analytic, but in dimension at least two no added point has a compact neighborhood. Small balls remain contractible, and explicit minimizing geodesics pass through added points. In dimension at least two, every fixed Fisher neighborhood of an added point has a positive minimax risk at every sample size. For subfamilies with a common positive lower bound on the variance in every nonzero integer direction, we prove that compactness of the completed closure is equivalent to uniform consistency in either $L^1$ or completed-Fisher loss. Equivalently, the Fourier moments must decay uniformly over the subfamily. A fixed covariance floor guarantees this condition without an upper covariance bound and gives $L^1$ risk at most $C_{\varepsilon,d}n^{-1/2}\{\log(n+1)\}^{d/4}$. A common-phase example illustrates how partially uniform limits can retain relative-phase information while remaining uniformly estimable.

We determine the intrinsic Fisher completion of the full-covariance wrapped Gaussian family on a fixed torus and relate its geometry to uniform estimation.
Statistical Uniformization of Surfaces by Heat-Kernel Fisher Information
Authors: Gunhee Cho, Hyun Chul Jang
First: 2026-10-27T16:42:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:42:17+00:00
Comments: 49 pages, 6 figures

Abstract
A Brownian particle observed at time $t$ carries information about where it started. On a closed surface this makes the surface itself a statistical model, whose Fisher information pulls back to a heat-smoothed metric $h_t=2tJ_t$; its volume $V_t$ is a normalized Jeffreys volume, and $\log(V_t/2t)$ is the model-dependent constant in Rissanen's minimum-description-length complexity. We ask whether maximizing this statistical size among metrics of fixed area in a conformal class recovers the uniformizing metric. First, $9(A-2πχt-V_t)/t^2$ converges to the curvature energy $\int K^2\,dA$ (the Calabi energy up to a topological constant), uniformly on classes of bounded geometry; hence, within a fixed-area conformal class of bounded geometry containing the uniformizer, approximate maximizers of $V_t$ converge to the constant-curvature metric (on the sphere, to the round metrics). Second, near every flat torus we prove a sharp finite-scale version: for small perturbations whose oscillations are resolved by the diffusion, $t|k|^2\leθ<τ_*$, the flat-subtracted defect is comparable to $\|Δf\|_2^2$, uniformly in the frequency cutoff and over noncollapsing lattices. The explicit threshold $τ_*=4.408\ldots$ is optimal, and it is exactly the observation time at which the information about one curvature mode is maximal. Third, the flat structure is seen only at an exponential scale: the volume loss of a flat torus measures the ambiguity of winding and is comparable to $t^{-1/2}e^{-\operatorname{sys}^2/(16t)}$, which selects the hexagonal lattice as $t\to0$ but not at all times.

The Assouad Dimension of Graphs: An Affirmative Answer to Fraser's Question
Authors: Binyan Yu, Bilel Selmi, Jia Liu, Yongshun Liang
First: 2026-10-27T16:06:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:06:51+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This article primarily investigates the Assouad dimension of graphs of continuous functions. Initially, the Assouad spectrum of the graph of a Hölder and count-Hölder continuous function has been derived. It has been shown that the Assouad dimension of the graph of a continuous function $f:[a,b]\to \mathbb{R}$ with the count-Hölder condition is at least \( 1 + \sup \left\{ \overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}L_f(y) : y \in \mathscr{R}_f[a,b]\right\} \) where $\overline{\dim}_{\mathrm{B}}$ denotes the upper box dimension, $L_f(y)$ is the level set of $f$ at level $y$, and $\mathscr{R}_f[a,b]$ is the range of $f$ on $[a,b]$. As a consequence, non-trivial lower bounds are obtained for the Assouad dimension of the graphs of classical functions such as the Weierstrass and Takagi functions. We have also employed a unified projection solely in the direction of the $y$-axis to establish lower and upper bounds for the Assouad dimension. On this basis, we have shown that the lower bound is sharp in terms of level sets, namely the upper box dimension cannot be replaced by the Assouad dimension. This provides a partial positive answer to Question 17.11.1 posed by Fraser in~\cite{Fraser2021}, and to Question 2.2 posed by Yu in~\cite{Yu2020}. Building on this result, the analysis has been further extended to a broader class of Weierstrass-type functions and some self-affine functions like fractal interpolation functions. Moreover, we have discussed certain circumstances under which the Assouad dimension can reach 2 with several examples constructed. A supplementary explanation of why Brownian motion has the full Assouad dimension has also been provided based on the preceding work of Howroyd and Yu \cite{Howroyd2019}.

This article primarily investigates the Assouad dimension of graphs of continuous functions.

Abstract

Abstract
We establish the variational characterization of the measure of maximal entropy on noncompact manifolds of nonpositive sectional curvature. Assuming that the deck transformation group contains an axial isometry with a rank-one axis and has a finite critical exponent, we prove that the supremum of the entropies of invariant probabilities equals the critical exponent and is attained if and only if the Bowen-Margulis-Sullivan (BMS) measure is finite. We also obtain mixing under a non-arithmetic length-spectrum assumption. For nonpositively curved surfaces of genus at least two, the existence of a closed Jacobi rank-one geodesic and the non-arithmeticity of the axial length spectrum are automatic.

Abstract
Let $\mathcal{N}(k,n)$ be the largest size of a family of affine $k$-planes in $\R^n$ at pairwise separation distance one. We develop a unified treatment of upper bounds, dimension reduction, and product constructions. Orthogonal-projection coordinates give a rank-stratified distance identity; a Veronese lifting converts its polynomial consequences into simultaneous total isotropy. Typical Kakeya-type sets and their typical code sets
Authors: Josef Bell, Ezekiel Fleissner-Kates, Tamás Keleti
First: 2026-10-27T15:35:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T15:35:48+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study properties of compact Besicovitch sets and other Kakeya-type sets which are typical in the sense that the exceptions form a meager set in the Baire category sense in a suitable metric space. The main motivation comes from the known fact that typical compact Besicovitch sets in $\mathbb{R}^n$ have minimal Hausdorff dimension among all Besicovitch sets of $\mathbb{R}^n$. Thus, in order to estimate the smallest possible Hausdorff dimension of an arbitrary Besicovitch set, it is enough to estimate the Hausdorff dimension of a typical (compact) Besicovitch set, or analogously of a Besicovitch set satisfying our list of typical properties. Among other results, we prove that a typical Besicovitch set $K$ in $\mathbb{R}^n$ has the following properties: it is not sticky, any line intersects $K$ in a set of ($1$-dimensional) measure zero or in a unit line segment and a set of measure zero, it has no more than $n$ different unit line segments in any direction, and for each $1\le j\le n$ the Hausdorff dimension of the set of those directions in which $K$ has exactly $j$ unit line segments is $n-j$. For compact metric spaces $X$ and $Y$, let $\mathcal{C}(X,Y)$ denote the set of compact subsets of $X\times Y$ whose projection onto the first coordinate is $X$, equipped with the Hausdorff metric. Kakeya-type compact sets can be coded by elements of such spaces, allowing typical properties to be studied through comeager subsets of $\mathcal C(X,Y)$. Among other results, we prove that, under suitable conditions on $X$ and $Y$, a typical $A\in \mathcal{C}(X,Y)$ is totally disconnected and purely unrectifiable, its projection to the second coordinate has Hausdorff dimension zero, and the dimension identities $\dim_H(A)=\dim_H(X)$ and $\dim_P(A)=\dim_P(X)+\dim Y$ hold.

Abstract
We provide a complex-geometric approach to the Blaschke conjecture, i.e., that a manifold whose injectivity radius equals its diameter is isometric to a compact rank-one symmetric space (CROSS). In particular, we introduce the great quadric fibration, a complex analogue of the great sphere fibration. Requiring its total space to be a complex submanifold simultaneously explains many properties that a Blaschke manifold is expected to possess, including its Clifford structure, Hopf fibration, and diffeomorphism class. Furthermore, the great quadric bundle coincides with the variety of minimal rational tangents (VMRT) of the ambient Fano variety in the standard CROSS cases, and we explain this through a bend-and-break argument in the setting of the Blaschke conjecture under a suitable complexification assumption. Combining this with Hwang-Mok VMRT recognition, we prove the Blaschke conjecture for manifolds admitting an adapted complex structure on their entire tangent bundles.

Statistics on Yau's conjecture: Variance asymptotics

First: 2026-07-10T19:57:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T15:31:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We investigate the probabilistic counterpart of Yau's conjecture on the nodal volume of Laplace eigenfunctions on compact manifolds, by studying the high-frequency variance asymptotics of Riemannian random waves. Theorem A gives quantitative bounds on the fluctuations of their nodal volume for all regimes of spectral windows, including the monochromatic one, of spectral size 1. For every non-monochromatic window the variance has the exact order $1/N$, where $N$ is the dimension of the underlying space of eigenfunctions, on any compact manifold. In the monochromatic regime, on manifolds without conjugate points and in particular on negatively curved ones, we prove that the variance is $o(1/N)$: this improves the previous best bound, cf. Canzani and Hanin 2020, to its square divided by a logarithmic factor, and exhibits a form of Berry's cancellation, relative to the natural scale $1/N$, for monochromatic random waves on such chaotic manifolds. Our proofs rely on a local and global analysis combining the Kac-Rice formula, the Wiener--Itô chaos decomposition recently obtained by the authors Stecconi and Todino 2025, and a sharp analysis of the error in the pointwise Weyl law associated to an arbitrary spectral window (Theorem B). We also introduce a general machinery (Theorem C) that yields variance decay for the nodal volume of an arbitrary Gaussian field under broad geometric conditions, subject to correlation decay assumptions.

Abstract

Abstract
We introduce a unified framework for supervised and unsupervised learning through density estimation using reproducing kernel Hilbert spaces (RKHSs). To this end, we introduce the notion of a nested RKHS, that is, an increasing sequence of RKHSs whose union is dense in an ambient $L^2$-space. This construction allows us to exploit the computational structure of RKHS methods at each finite approximation level while preserving the approximation power of the entire $L^2$-space. We first study statistical learning in a fixed RKHS. In both unsupervised and supervised settings, we establish strong convergence results for empirical averages of random elements taking values in an RKHS continuously embedded in $L^2$. We then use the nested RKHS structure to combine these statistical convergence results with the density of the union of the RKHSs in the ambient $L^2$-space. We apply this construction to probability density estimation and binary Bayesian classification. For density estimation, we construct explicit empirical estimators of the orthogonal projections of the target density onto the RKHSs and establish their almost sure convergence. A diagonal selection procedure then yields a sequence of estimators converging almost surely to the target density in $L^2$. For binary Bayesian classification, we formulate the approximation of the Bayes target as the minimization of suitable population and empirical risk functionals. We prove consistency of the empirical minimizers and use the nested structure to obtain convergence towards the Bayes target in $L^2$. We further provide a geometric interpretation in terms of hyperplanes and weighted distances in the ambient $L^2$-space. Finally, we show that the population risk admits a direct probabilistic interpretation in terms of the probability of misclassification.

Abstract
Deep learning algorithms have become widely used and successful across a wide range of tasks. Abstract
Already in $\mathbb{R}^4$, there are many minimal hypersurfaces but few structural results. The half-space theorem fails there: higher-dimensional catenoids are among the many examples contained in a slab. Nevertheless, we show that in any dimension and codimension, the height of a minimal submanifold controls its volume growth: confinement to a narrow cone forces polynomial growth, and sublinear height forces Euclidean growth, the slowest possible. Thus volume bounds, which regularity theory assumes, follow from height alone. The key is a volume doubling theorem that converts geometric confinement into quantitative rigidity: if an $n$-dimensional stationary integral varifold lies in a thin slab at a given scale, then its volume doubling ratio at that scale is bounded by a universal constant. Iterating this gives the polynomial growth bounds above. Sublinear height is exactly the borderline for Euclidean growth: examples grow at least like $r^{n+4ε^2/5}$ in cones of aperture $ε$. From Euclidean volume growth, we obtain an optimal Bernstein theorem, in all dimensions, for stable minimal hypersurfaces with sublinear height growth, generalizing results of Moser, Bombieri--De Giorgi--Miranda, Trudinger, Caffarelli--Nirenberg--Spruck, and Ecker--Huisken.

Abstract
We study how a functional inequality satisfied by the one-particle conditional distributions of a Gibbs measure, with a constant independent of the positions of the other particles, can be propagated to the whole N -particle system, with a constant independent of N , when the interaction is singular. Our model is the Coulomb gas on the torus T 3 in the mean-field regime. We distinguish two mechanisms. The first one is a dependent tensorization, governed by Dobrushin-Wu interdependence coefficients. It yields uniform Poincar{é} and transport-information W 2 I inequalities with fully explicit constants. We explain why this mechanism does not extend to the log-Sobolev inequality: the Zegarlinski-type coefficients, which require pointwise bounds on the interaction, are infinite. The second mechanism is a tensorization with a defect. Using a uniform bound on the partition function due to Duerinckx and Jabin, it yields a uniform log-Sobolev inequality for the Coulomb gas on T 3 at small charge. We identify the assumptions on the interaction under which each mechanism applies. All the results are then extended to the two-dimensional Coulomb gas and to periodic Riesz gases 1/|x|^s , 0 < s $\le$ 1. The extension to d $\ge$ 4 is also discussed.
