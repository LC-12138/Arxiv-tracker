On the Gabor frame set of polynomial-Gaussian windows
Authors: Jakob Lemvig
First: 2026-11-03T18:38:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T18:38:40+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We prove two sufficient conditions for the frame property of Gabor systems $\mathcal{G}(g,a,b)=\{e^{2πi mb\,\cdot}g(\cdot-ak):k,m\in\mathbb{Z} \}$ generated by polynomial--Gaussian windows $g(t)=P(t)e^{-ct^2}$, where $c>0$ and $P$ is a nonzero complex polynomial of degree $n$. The conditions depend only on the product $ab$ of the lattice parameters and the degree of $P$. First, $\mathcal{G}(g,a,b)$ is a frame whenever $ab<1$ is irrational. Second, if $ab=p/q<1$, with $p,q\in\mathbb{N}$ coprime, then $\mathcal{G}(g,a,b)$ is a frame whenever $q-p>n$. Consequently, in the region $ab<1$, failure of the frame property can occur only on rational hyperbolas whose reduced denominator and numerator differ by at most $n$. The proof extends the translated-Wronskian method of Faulhuber and Petersen [\emph{The frame set of the first Hermite function}, arXiv:2609.02610] from the first Hermite function to arbitrary polynomial multiples of a Gaussian, combining a multiplicity estimate for Wronskians with Jensen's formula.

Toric gravitational instantons with four fixed points
Authors: Lars Andersson, Mattias Dahl, Gustav Nilsson
First: 2026-11-03T18:35:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T18:35:19+00:00
Comments: 47 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We classify the simply connected toric gravitational instantons whose torus action has four fixed points. They are the hyperkähler multi-Eguchi--Hanson and multi-Taub--NUT metrics with four collinear centres, the asymptotically flat instantons on Li and Sun's manifold $X_3$, up to scale one for each twist at infinity in $(0,1)$ for a fixed orientation of the torus, and, up to scale, a single further instanton with an ALF end, which is not hyperkähler. The last two are Hermitian for neither orientation. In particular, the toric asymptotically flat instanton on $X_3$ with a given twist is unique up to scale, as conjectured by Li and Sun. The proof combines the solitonic form of Geroch's conjecture, which turns smoothness into polynomial equations for the soliton data, with a convexity principle adapted from Aksteiner. At a fixed asymptotic parameter, the cone errors of the conical metrics with a given rod structure are the gradient of a strictly convex function of the rod lengths, and the strict convexity gives uniqueness. Existence follows from an explicit solution for the ALF end and, for the AF ends, from Li and Sun's existence theorem. We also outline how a chart of Aksteiner identifies these AF instantons with those found by Teo.

Abstract
In this article, we investigate inequalities involving the min-max width associated to the distance function and the area of two-dimensional Riemannian spheres. A Linear Lower Bound for Convex Body Chasing in Every Norm
Authors: Nicolas Christianson
First: 2026-10-23T14:12:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T17:31:39+00:00
Comments: Updates the previous version to extend the lower bound to all norms
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We establish an $Ω(d)$ lower bound on the competitive ratio of convex body chasing in $\mathbb{R}^d$ with movement costs measured in any norm. Previously, the best known lower bound valid across all norms was $Ω(d^{1/6})$, established by Papazov (2023); stronger lower bounds were known for $\ell_p$ norms, where Bubeck, Klartag, Lee, Li, and Sellke (2019) had established a lower bound of $Ω(\max\{\sqrt{d}, d^{1-1/p}\})$. In particular, a linear lower bound was known only for the $\ell_\infty$ norm, realized by the standard construction of chasing faces of a hypercube. Our lower bound strictly improves on the prior universal and $\ell_p$ lower bounds for $p < \infty$ and shows that the functional Steiner point algorithm of Sellke (2023) - which has competitive ratio $O(d)$ - is asymptotically optimal for every norm. Our approach is based on the recursive construction of Bubeck, Coester, and Rabani (2023) for metrical service systems, which gives a lower bound of $Ω(r^2)$ for requests of size $O(r)$. We show that requests in their construction, each comprising a set of discrete points in a graph metric, can be mapped to the vertices of convex requests in a $(2r+1)$-dimensional $\ell_p$ vector space while approximately preserving movement costs up to a factor of $O(r)$; the general result then follows via Milman's (1985) quotient-of-a-subspace theorem.

First: 2026-10-14T12:27:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T16:59:15+00:00
Comments: Superseded by arXiv:2611.03277 (joint with S. Sabourau). Not intended for publication
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Let $Γ$ be a connected metric graph of first Betti number $b\ge 2$, and let $Γ_b$ be a trivalent graph of the same Betti number with all edges of unit length. We show that if $\text{length}(Γ) \le λ\, \text{length}(Γ_b)$, then the universal cover of $Γ$ contains a vertex around which, at every radius $R\ge 0$, the ball is at least as long as the ball of radius $R$ in the trivalent tree whose edges all have length $3λ$. In particular, for $λ\le\frac13$, this ball is at least as long as the ball of the same radius in the universal cover of $Γ_b$, with no multiplicative loss. This improves an earlier theorem of the author, which gives the weaker lower bound $(1-3λ)$ times the ball in the universal cover of $Γ_b$, and only for $λ\le\frac16$. The proof rests on an elementary comparison principle. Among trees whose vertices have degree at least three and whose edges have length at most $c$, the trivalent tree with all edges of length $c$ has the smallest balls at every radius. As an application, we double the exponential growth rate in the author's earlier uniform lower bound for the area of balls in the universal cover of small-area surfaces of hyperbolic type, from $\ln 2$ to $2\ln 2$, beyond the hyperbolic rate. We also obtain explicit constants in the positive answer to Guth's question for surfaces. Finally, we conjecture the sharp, all-scale version of the minimal volume entropy inequality of Kapovich-Nagnibeda and Lim, and show that it would be optimal.

Abstract
We give an elementary proof of a tangent-cone identity which might be known to specialists in geometric measure theory. It concerns Lipschitz mappings $g$ from the closed unit ball $B\subset\mathbb{R}^2$ to $\mathbb{R}^2$. Let $x$ be an interior point of differentiability of $g$ at which $Dg(x)$ has rank one. If the upper one-dimensional Hausdorff density of $g(B)$ at $g(x)$ is at most one, then the tangent cone to $g(B)$ at $g(x)$ coincides with the image of $Dg(x)$. This property is used in the proof of rigidity for the four-gradient problem. We provide a detailed, self-contained proof based on differentiability, connectedness and elementary estimates for one-dimensional Hausdorff measure, without using advanced methods from geometric measure theory.

Abstract
We investigate the structure of kernels of finite-rank perturbations of truncated Toeplitz operators. By realizing a perturbed truncated Toeplitz operator on a model space as a suitable matrix-valued truncated Toeplitz operator on an enlarged model space, we identify its kernel with a coordinate projection of the kernel of an associated block Toeplitz operator. Abstract
Let $M$ be a smooth manifold of dimension $n\ge4$, and let $F$ be a smooth, strongly convex Landsberg metric, regular on the entire slit tangent bundle. We prove that $F$ is Berwald if, at each fixed base point, the Minkowski norm is real-analytic on the entire slit tangent space. The proof rests on two analytic rigidity theorems for a single Minkowski space. The first asserts that, in dimension at least $3$, a real-analytic Minkowski norm whose Hessian metric is flat is induced by an inner product. The second asserts that, in dimension at least $4$, the Hessian metric of a real-analytic Minkowski norm is flat if the entire indicatrix carries a nonzero smooth symmetric quartic form satisfying a twisted Codazzi equation. The quartic form need not be real-analytic, and the second theorem is proved independently of the first. Applying these results to the angular Berwald quartic form shows that the metric is Riemannian on the open set of base points on whose fibres the Berwald curvature is not identically zero. Since every Riemannian metric is Berwald, this set must be empty. No analytic structure, compactness or completeness of the base manifold is assumed, and the metric need not be reversible.

Abstract
We investigate the convex-body containment problem $\max\{s >0 : s Z \subseteq Q\}$, where the outer body $Q \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ is described by a membership oracle and the inner body $Z \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ is a zonotope. Abstract

Abstract
For a bounded sequence $y=(y_n)\subset E^*$ and $p>0$, put $G_p(y)=\{x\in E:(y_n(x))\in\ell_p\}$ and call $y$ $p$-strongly normal when $G_p(y)$ is dense in $E$. The possible subsequence and block spectra are exactly $\varnothing$, $(0,\infty)$, $(1,\infty)$, $[r,\infty)$, and $(r,\infty)$ for $r>1$, and their possible pairs are precisely the nested pairs. Without a basicness requirement, the possible original-sequence spectra are all upper subsets of $(0,\infty)$; each is realized in a separable Hilbert space. We determine the exact universal perturbation classes: $\ell_p$ for a fixed original-sequence exponent, $c_0$ for the subsequence spectrum, and $\ell_1$ for the block spectrum. The ideal-valued version holds for every proper solid permutation-invariant linear ideal $c_{00}\subset A\subsetneq c_0$, without a norm or completeness assumption. For the author's 2012 selection model, the bounded-run criterion extends to all proper symmetric quasi-Banach sequence ideals. In the unbounded-run case, a single norm-one projection annihilates the summability domains of any family of fewer than $\mathfrak b$ such ideals; the quotient by their closed linear span contains a $1$-complemented isometric copy of $\ell_\infty$. Such copies also occur in the left jump at every $q>1$ and in the right jump at every $q\ge1$ of the scale of closed summability domains. Thus the common density criterion coexists with two-sided complemented discontinuities.
