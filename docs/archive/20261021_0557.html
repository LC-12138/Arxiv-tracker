Elastic knots: compactness, contact, and variational structure
Authors: Philipp Reiter, Heiko von der Mosel
First: 2026-10-19T17:54:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:54:19+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study bending-minimizing sequences of closed curves in a fixed knot class and the self-contact of their limits. A positive matrix-valued measure records a possible loss of curvature energy. Ambient variations, localized on arbitrary coincidence sets, force this measure to vanish and give strong convergence in $H^2$. We then derive the elastic equation on isolated strands and overlap bundles, describe the distribution that remains at contact, and prove balance and rigidity statements under their precise additional hypotheses. Equality in the bridge bound gives constant curvature. Combining strong compactness with quantitative competitors gives an alternative proof that elastic limits in classes with equal bridge and braid indices are covered circles. An independent recovery theorem identifies the strong $H^2$ closure with the regular $H^2$ part of the $C^1$ closure and also implies strong compactness. Finally, a whole-line contact model illustrates which estimates are needed for finer shape selection.

Abstract
On the real hyperbolic space $\mathbb{H}^n$, the classical Muckenhoupt condition does not describe the weights $w$ for which the Hardy-Littlewood maximal operator $M$ is bounded on $L^p(w)$. We show that, for $1<p<\infty$, $M$ is bounded on $L^p(w)$ if and only if $w$ is a local $A_p$ weight and satisfies a two-layer condition, which compares $w$ and its dual weight on two layers of the shadow of a point. We also treat the weak type case, radial weights and the range $p\le1$, and we prove a weighted Herz principle for radial convolution operators.

Tight lower bounds for Lipschitz geodesically convex optimization on hyperbolic space
Authors: Christopher Criscitiello
First: 2026-10-19T17:51:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:51:34+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We determine the dimension-independent first-order query complexity of minimizing a Lipschitz geodesically convex (g-convex) function on hyperbolic space. Given a ball of radius $r$ containing a global minimizer, we prove that $Ω(ζ_r/ε^2)$ queries are necessary to attain accuracy $εMr$, where $M$ is the Lipschitz constant and $ζ_r=r/\tanh r$ captures the effect of curvature. This matches the known $O(ζ_r/ε^2)$ upper bound for the Riemannian subgradient method (Zhang and Sra, 2016). This is the first query-complexity result for g-convex optimization that is tight simultaneously in curvature and target accuracy. The standard Euclidean recipe of constructing hard instances as maxima of affine functions does not transfer to hyperbolic space, so the lower bound requires a new mechanism. Our starting point is the worst-function construction of Criscitiello and Boumal (2023, Appendix A). It gives the same lower bound for a subclass of algorithms, including the subgradient method, whose queries remain in prescribed g-convex regions. We retain this geometric setup and carefully tune and augment their hard function to accommodate every deterministic first-order algorithm. The key new mechanism is an advance-or-stall scheme: when a query lies in these regions, the oracle reveals at most one new information direction (advance); when a query leaves them, we show that the oracle can answer without revealing any new hidden direction (stall). Unlike earlier curvature-dependent lower bounds, the proof uses no ball-packing argument.

We determine the dimension-independent first-order query complexity of minimizing a Lipschitz geodesically convex (g-convex) function on hyperbolic space.

Authors: Chenxing Qian

Abstract
We study constrained quantization of a compactly supported probability $P$ on $\mathbb{R}^D$ by codebooks in a nonempty closed set $S$. For $0<r\le\infty$, the constrained error $e_{n,r}$ is attained and satisfies $e_{n,r}\downarrow L_r=\|\operatorname{dist}(\cdot,S)\|_{L^r(P)}$. Set $Δ_{n,r}=e_{n,r}^r-L_r^r$ for finite $r$. A Borel nearest-point selector $T$ induces $μ=T_*P$. Writing $q_{n,s}$ for the classical $L^s$ quantization error, we obtain $e_{n,r}-L_r\le2q_{n,r}(μ)$ and $Δ_{n,r}\le C_rq_{n,1}(μ)$ for $1\le r<\infty$, with $C_r$ independent of $n$, and $Δ_{n,r}\le2^rq_{n,r}(μ)^r$ for $0<r<1$. Also, $e_{n,\infty}-L_\infty\le2c_n(E)$, where $E$ is the nearest-point image of $\operatorname{supp}P$ and $c_n(E)$ is its covering radius. For $1\le r<\infty$, a weighted power-growth condition with exponent $β>0$ on a fixed source set $F$ bounds $Δ_{n,r}$ below by the classical $β$-power quantization cost of $ν_F=T_*(wP|_F)$. If $dν_F=\widehat w\,dη$ for a compactly supported reference probability $η$ and $\widehat w\in A_p(η)$, $1<p<\infty$, then $Δ_{n,r}\geν_F(\mathbb{R}^D)[\widehat w]_{A_p(η)}^{-1}q_{n,β/p}(η)^β$. These comparisons yield bounds for the quantization dimensions defined by $e_{n,r}-L_r$ and $Δ_{n,r}^{1/r}$, with distinct behavior at positive and zero baselines. Convex and uniformly prox-regular constraints supply the growth weights through their projection inequalities. Circle and lifted Cantor distributions give matching quadratic-excess rates, while rectifiable weighted projections give lower Zador estimates.

Abstract
In this paper, we prove a uniform pseudolocality theorem for smooth complete Ricci flows whose initial metrics have Ricci curvature bounded below and uniformly positive injectivity radius. No a priori curvature bound is assumed on the flow. As a consequence, we obtain strong uniqueness for smooth complete Ricci flows from a complete metric with the above geometric bounds. We also prove that every smooth complete Ricci flow from a complete Ricci-flat metric with uniformly positive injectivity radius is stationary. The same conclusion holds for any complete flat initial metric. In particular, we give an affirmative answer to Chen's question on the strong uniqueness of Ricci flow from Euclidean space in every dimension.

The moduli space of quasimorphisms on subshifts and its pressure geometry
Authors: Stephen Cantrell, Pablo Carrasco, Emilio Corso, Federico Rodriguez Hertz
First: 2026-10-19T17:30:55+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T17:30:55+00:00
Comments: 52 pages, 1 table. Comments are welcome
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We introduce a metric geometry on the space of positive, linearly growing quasimorphisms on a mixing subshift of finite type. Two such quasimorphisms are identified when their homogenizations agree up to multiplicative scaling. The resulting moduli space carries a natural symmetrised Thurston-type metric, defined from the extremal ratios of their periodic orbit lengths. We prove that this is a genuine metric and determine several global features of the resulting space: it is unbounded, separable, incomplete, and has geodesics that are canonically generated by thermodynamic pressure functions. We then describe the endpoints of these geodesics and develop a boundary theory for our moduli space. We apply this theory to metric structures on right-angled Artin and Coxeter groups. In particular we construct isometric embeddings of geometric moduli spaces into our quasimorphism moduli spaces. As a consequence, Manhattan curves for metrics on these groups are $C^1$, with equality cases characterised by rough similarity of the underlying metrics. This provides insights into Manhattan curves in settings where neither classical thermodynamic formalism for Hölder potentials nor Patterson-Sullivan theory apply. The same construction recovers the corresponding results for hyperbolic groups and places these examples within a common dynamical-geometric framework.

Abstract
We derive a canonical functional model for a pair of isometric operators $(V_1, V_2)$ acting on a Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ that satisfy the automorphic covariance relation $V_1 V_2 = V_2 \varphi(V_1)$, where $\varphi$ is an automorphism of the unit disk $\mathbb{D}$. This extends the classical Bercovici--Douglas--Foias functional model for commuting isometric pairs to a dynamically twisted setting. When both $V_1$ and $V_2$ are pure isometries (shifts), the model simplifies to $(M_z, M_Θ W_\varphi^*)$ acting on $H^2(\mathcal{F})$, where $W_\varphi$ is a unitary weighted composition operator on the Hardy space $H^2$ and $Θ\in H^\infty(\mathbb{D}, \mathcal{B}(\mathcal{F}))$ is an operator-valued inner function such that $M_Θ$ is a shift. As an application, we establish a complete unitary classification theorem for automorphically covariant isometric pairs by identifying a complete set of joint unitary invariants. Notably, our results are novel even in the special case of rotation automorphisms ($V_1 V_2 = q V_2 V_1$ with $|q| = 1$).

Abstract
Let $A\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a nonempty compact set and let $K\subset\mathbb{R}^n$ be a nonempty compact convex set. We study the problem of when there exists $λ>0$ such that the Minkowski sum $A+λK$ is convex. First, we show that this finite convexification is equivalent to a uniform covering condition on the exposed sets of $A$ and $K$. We then show that another characterization is possible in terms of the covering radii of the iterated exposed sets after translating each exposed face of $K$ so that its relative centroid is $0$. In the case of convex polytopes, we show that it suffices to check the exposed sets in the first level of the iteration. We also study several special cases, such as strictly convex convexifiers, lower-dimensional convexifiers, strongly isomorphic polytopes, and the planar case.

Curvature distributions and the Steiner formula for characteristic surfaces in the Heisenberg group
Authors: Davide Barilari, Eugenio Bellini, Tommaso Rossi
First: 2026-10-19T16:41:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T16:41:43+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We establish a new Steiner-type formula for smooth surfaces in the sub-Riemannian Heisenberg group. The main novelty is that we admit characteristic points, whose presence produces nontrivial singularities both of the distance from the surface and of its tubular neighborhood. Such singularities are reflected in the Steiner formula: the expansion up to a given order $N\geq 4$ of the volume of the $\varepsilon$-tubular neighborhood $\mathcal T_S(\varepsilon)$ of a surface $S$ is no longer polynomial, in general, taking the form \[ \mathrm{vol}(\mathcal T_S(\varepsilon)) = \sum_{j=1}^3α_j\varepsilon^j+\sum_{n=4}^N \left(α_n+β_n\log\varepsilon\right)\varepsilon^n+o(\varepsilon^N),\qquad\text{as }\varepsilon\to0^+ . \] More precisely, under a geometric stability condition on characteristic points, we prove that the first three coefficients are integrals over $S$ of locally integrable curvature densities, coinciding with those known in the non-characteristic case. A new phenomenon appears at order $4$, equal to the homogeneous dimension of the Heisenberg group, where logarithmic terms may occur. We show that the higher-order coefficients are naturally described by a distributional analogue of Federer's curvature measures, whose logarithmic components are supported on the characteristic set. We compute the fourth-order coefficients explicitly. The proof is based on different ingredients of independent interest: (i) an explicit formula for the cut time of homogeneous surfaces, (ii) a fine analysis of the normal exponential map through blow-ups, (iii) the study of the stability of the surface cut locus under perturbations.

We establish a new Steiner-type formula for smooth surfaces in the sub-Riemannian Heisenberg group.

Exponential improvements in Rado's covering problem
Authors: Gian Maria Dall'Ara, Adrian Dumitrescu
First: 2026-09-21T18:32:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-19T16:20:00+00:00
Comments: In this version, the algorithmic version has been expanded and a few minor revisions have been made
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Let $B^d$ denote the $d$-dimensional Euclidean ball of unit radius. What is the largest constant $f(B^d) \in [0,1]$ with the property that every finite collection $\mathcal{C}$ of unit balls in $\mathbb{R}^d$ admits a disjoint sub-collection $\mathcal{S}$ occupying at least a fraction $f(B^d)$ of the volume of $\mathcal{C}$? This problem was first raised by T. Radó in 1928, for axis-parallel squares in the plane; the author was motivated by a classical covering lemma in real analysis due to Vitali. The case of Euclidean balls was first considered by R. Rado in 1949. Until last year the best known estimates on $f(B^d)$ for unit balls where very far apart: \[ (1+ε_d) 3^{-d} \leq f(B^d) \leq 2^{-d}, \] where $0<ε_d=o_{d\rightarrow \infty}(1)$. Recently, the authors of this note observed that an exponential improvement on the upper bound follows from the Kabatiansky--Levenshtein spherical code bound, while the lower bound was improved by a linear factor by C.~Xie and G.~Ge (see arxiv:2608.09744). The current best estimates for large $d$ are \[ c \cdot d \cdot 3^{-d} \leq f(B^d) \leq 2.447^{-d}, \] where $c>0$ is an absolute constant. Here we offer the first exponential improvement of the lower bound in almost 80 years, which narrows the gap to: \[ 2.910^{-d} \leq f(B^d) \leq 2.447^{-d}. \] Our method is constructive and yields a polynomial time algorithm for finding a disjoint sub-collection realizing the estimate. Moreover the same technique gives similar exponentially improved lower bounds for all symmetric convex bodies satisfying a uniform convexity assumption, e.g., $\ell^p$-balls for all $p\in (1,\infty)$.

Abstract
We continue the study of the octonionic spectrum of the octonionic structure operator $h$ on $W=\mathbb O\otimes_{\mathbb R}V$, where $V$ is the seven-dimensional representation of $G_2$. The real spectrum and the octonionic one-step spectrum, given by the equation $h(w)=wλ$ for real or octonion $λ$, have been determined in earlier work. In this paper, we study the two-step equation $h(w)=(wλ_1)λ_2$. Due to non-associativity, this does not in general reduce to the one-step problem. An adapted quaternionic frame represents successive right multiplication by an operator with four real coefficients. The $SU(2)$ subgroup fixing the frame splits $W$ into invariant sectors of dimensions $16$, $12$ and $28$. We factor the determinant of the resulting 4-parameter operator pencil and characterize the pairs for which a nonzero solution exists. The singular locus of this pencil in $\mathbb R^4$ is the union of an explicit algebraic set and a round $3$-sphere. The $12$-dimensional sector carries the one-step problem at the reversed product $λ_2λ_1$. The $28$-dimensional sector contributes no additional real singular points; the fixed sector produces solutions even when neither product lies in the one-step right spectrum.

Abstract
