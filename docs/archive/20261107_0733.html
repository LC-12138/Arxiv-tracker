Abstract
In this paper, we prove that every complete noncompact two-sided properly embedded constant mean curvature hypersurface of finite Morse index in Euclidean space is minimal. The result holds in every dimension. We assume no bounds on curvature or volume growth. This establishes the properly embedded case of the finite-index version of do Carmo's question. For nonzero mean curvature, we use the radii of inscribed balls to define a positive weight. This weight gives a polynomial bound on weighted volume, while its reciprocal satisfies a differential inequality involving the Jacobi operator. We combine these properties with stability outside a compact set to obtain finite total area. This leads to a contradiction.

Abstract
The goal of this paper is to investigate the following question: what geometric restrictions does the existence of a transitive family of constant mean curvature hyperspheres impose on a Riemannian metric on $\mathbb S^n$? The notion of a transitive family can be made precise through the theory of Zoll families developed by Ambrozio, Marques, and Neves. Finally, we also investigate inverse problems in the spirit of Hangan, asking which metrics make a fixed family of geodesic spheres have a common prescribed mean curvature.

Abstract
Discovering continuous symmetries directly from differential-equation data can reveal geometric structure for model reduction and scientific machine learning without symbolic determining equations or a closed-form representation of the governing system. We formulate intrinsic Lie point-symmetry discovery as an operator-theoretic problem on sampled jet-space data. Admissible generators are identified from a sampled determining operator and separated from redundant, solution-dependent, and geometrically trivial directions. The framework combines prolongation, sequential subspace restrictions, and a fixed nonlinear dictionary linear in trainable coefficients. A rank-stabilization preflight test diagnoses systems whose represented intrinsic symmetry algebra does not stabilize under dictionary refinement. On a controlled differential-equation benchmark with an analytically known 11-dimensional represented intrinsic symmetry algebra, the method recovers the complete represented generator set, reproduces its Lie-bracket structure to numerical precision, and approximates a known generator beyond the training domain. Here we show that explicit intrinsic continuous-symmetry generators can be recovered from data obtained from exact or approximate differential systems.

Spectral Regimes of Truncated sinc-Power and Pulse-Train Kernels: Edge Exponents, Per-Edge Plunges, and the Landau-Widom Limit
Authors: Unnikrishna Pillai
First: 2026-11-05T18:11:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-05T18:11:38+00:00
Comments: 26 pages, 10 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A signal band-limited to one rectangular band and observed through a finite window is the classical concentration problem of Slepian, Landau, Pollak, and Widom. The eigenvalues of the operator cluster near one and near zero, separated by a transition called the plunge. The plunge width grows like the logarithm of the window length, with a universal coefficient of one over pi squared set by the band-edge jump. This paper follows the clusters and the plunge as the band is deformed. An elementwise power of the kernel smooths the band edge, and the plunge gives way to a power-law edge with exponent one less than the power. Disjoint copies of the band widen the plunge in proportion to the number of band edges, as the multiple-interval theorem of Landau and Widom implies, so the coefficient is a per-edge constant. Shrinking each band to a line gives the classical finite-rank operator. Overlapping bands give a multilevel staircase, and a self-similar band gives a cluster hierarchy governed by the Cantor dimension. At the smooth edge, rescaled eigenvalues do not collapse onto one curve. The edge descriptor passes from a constant to an exponent to an integer to a fractal dimension. For the single band, classical results place all eigenfilter zeros on the unit circle, with more of them inside the band as the order rises, so the dominant eigenvectors act as lowpass filters.

Abstract

Abstract
Eldan's level-$1{:}3$ inequality is a central tool in his second-order correlation inequalities for increasing Boolean functions. His proof is going to Gaussian space and uses an entropy estimate for second Hermite tensors. We give a direct proof on the discrete hypercube. Abstract

We construct block-diagonal compact operators for which this implication fails. For von Neumann algebras with separable predual, we prove that this cancellation property is equivalent to subhomogeneity. Moreover, we obtain a cancellation law for finite ampliations in unital Banach algebras when the commutant of one element is finite dimensional modulo its Jacobson radical.

Abstract
We study the $L^p$ concentration problem for the Born-Jordan distribution in dimension $d>1$, thus extending the one-dimensional analysis in [Stra-Svela-Trapasso, J. Math. Pures Appl. (2026)]. We show that the existence of concentration optimizers depends on the exponent $p$ with a critical threshold at $p_*(d)= \frac{2d}{d-2}$ for $d\geq2$ (with the understanding that $p_*(2)=\infty$). In particular, for subcritical exponents $1\leq p<p_*(d)$ we prove that the supremum is finite and is attained, whereas for supercritical exponents $p>p_*(d)$ we show that the functional is unbounded. We also provide the complete solution in the (significantly more) challenging critical regime in dimension $d=2$. For the critical case in dimension $d=2$ and the supercritical case in dimension $d>2$ we also show that $L^2$ functions whose Born-Jordan distribution does not belong to $L^p$ are generic in the Baire sense. For the critical exponent in dimension $d>2$, for which Wang has recently shown that the supremum is finite, we prove that the functional is not sequentially weakly upper semicontinuous, showing that the still open question of attainment is nontrivial.
