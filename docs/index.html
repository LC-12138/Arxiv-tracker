Abstract
The paper is devoted to the construction of exact general solutions of the equations of oil filtration in a porous medium under the action of an active reagent injected into the reservoir. The choice of the reagent solution determines the control process of chemical oil flooding in hard-to-recover fields. The problem is described by a system of two first-order nonlinear partial differential equations (the Barenblatt model). Conditions are found on the Buckley--Leverett function, the adsorption function, and the distribution function of the reagent in oil under which the original system, by means of a nonlinear change of variables on a five-dimensional manifold endowed with a contact structure, is reduced to a linear wave system with constant coefficients. Six families of such functions are indicated. For them, contact transformations and Legendre surfaces corresponding to general solutions in implicit form are constructed.

Coarse Embeddings Beyond Doubling: Persistence Diagrams and Multiparameter Modules
Authors: Sushovan Majhi, Atish Mitra, Žiga Virk
First: 2026-11-09T18:53:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-09T18:53:15+00:00
Comments: 23 pages, 3 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study coarse embeddings into Hilbert space and the asymptotic dimension of spaces of persistence diagrams and multiparameter persistence modules. We replace doubling, the usual hypothesis for landmark embeddings, by admissible tent covers of bounded multiplicity at every scale: stacking them across dyadic scales gives an explicit $1$-Lipschitz coarse embedding, with lower gauge $c\sqrt{τt}$, or nearly linear for slowly decaying weights, and the same covers bound the asymptotic dimension by the multiplicity. The spaces of persistence diagrams are not doubling but carry such covers, and for diagrams with $n$ points our covers improve the constant of the known landmark embedding by a factor exponential in $n$. Slice by slice, the construction embeds multiparameter modules with boundedly many bars per slice under the matching distance on any finite net of slices, a class that contains the modules with at most $n$ generators. Bounding bars or persistent Betti numbers instead of generators fails: two-parameter modules with one bar on every slice have infinite asymptotic dimension, and neither they nor the modules of bounded support and bounded rank admit a coarse embedding into Hilbert space, under the matching or the interleaving distance.

The Brown measure of a sum of two free nonselfadjoint random variables, one of which is R-diagonal
Authors: Hari Bercovici, Yu Kitagawa, Ping Zhong
First: 2022-09-26T02:32:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-09T18:44:27+00:00
Comments: 50 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Suppose that $X_1$ and $X_2$ are two $*$-free, possibly unbounded, random variables with Brown measures $μ_{X_1}$ and $μ_{X_2}$ respectively. Using properties of classic free additive convolution, we develop a method for calculating $μ_{X_1+X_2}$ when $X_2$ is $R$-diagonal. This method determines a density relative to Lebesgue measure on an open set whose closure equals the support of $μ_{X_{1}+X_{2}}$. We prove that $μ_{X_1+X_2}$ has no singular continuous part, that its density is strictly positive on this open set, and we determine the masses of its atoms. Explicit calculations are possible in several important cases. As an application, we combine our Fuglede--Kadison determinant formula with known subordination convergence results to calculate the limiting logarithmic potentials of free multiplicative random walks. We identify the limit with the Fuglede--Kadison determinant formula for free multiplicative Brownian motion previously proved by some PDE methods.

Delaunay Asymptotics for the Singular Escobar-Yamabe Equation
Authors: Felipe G. Bogo
First: 2026-11-09T18:38:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-09T18:38:58+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In dimensions $n\geq3$, we prove that near an isolated boundary singularity a positive solution of the Escobar--Yamabe equation for a smooth metric approaches a fixed periodic Delaunay solution or the cylindrical solution if its trace lies between positive multiples of $|\overline x|^{-(n-2)/2}$, where $\overline x$ is the tangential Fermi coordinate. The relative error decays at a positive power rate, with weighted estimates through two derivatives in Fermi coordinates. The necksize is unique, and the translation parameter is unique modulo the least positive period in the periodic case. The Hamiltonian smoothly parametrizes the periodic family modulo translation. We classify Jacobi fields of polynomial growth at both ends without assuming symmetry. Translation and energy differentiation generate these fields along periodic solutions, and two oscillatory fields generate them along the cylindrical solution. We also exhibit a first-order obstruction to improving these asymptotics by boundary-preserving conformal deformations of the flat models.

Abstract
We establish a symmetry principle for asymptotically cylindrical steady gradient Ricci solitons and asymptotically conical expanding gradient Ricci solitons. Our symmetry principle bounds the loss of symmetry from the asymptotic link to the interior by the dimension of the weighted $L^2$ kernel of the stability operator. For homogeneous links, suitable kernel bounds imply that the soliton has an isometric action of cohomogeneity one. The results apply in particular to strictly linear stable solitons, require no curvature sign assumptions, and allow orbifold singular sets of codimension at least four. Applications include characterizations of Bryant solitons and global $U(m)$-symmetry results for solitons with quotient-Berger asymptotic links. In dimension four, we classify asymptotically $SU(2)/Γ$-cylindrical steady gradient Ricci solitons with trivial weighted kernel as finite quotients of Bryant or Appleton solitons. Under the same kernel condition, we also establish toric symmetry for asymptotically lens-cylindrical steady solitons.

Abstract
We study the one-parameter family of Riemannian metrics $g_λ$ induced by the Hamiltonian cotangent deformation $H_λ= H_0 + \fracλ{2}\|J\|^2$ on simply connected space forms of constant curvature $κ\in \{1, 0, -1\}$, where $J$ is the momentum map of the isotropy action fixing a base point. Extensions to anisotropic sphere families and intrinsic deformations of compact rank-one symmetric spaces ($\mathbb{C}P^n, \mathbb{H}P^n, \mathbb{O}P^2$) are also established.

Global Green-Schwarz response on quaternionic sphere bundles
Authors: Yuqi Li, Hao-Yu Sun
First: 2026-11-09T18:16:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-09T18:16:30+00:00
Comments: 86 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Local anomaly polynomials describe local variations, but a normalized Green-Schwarz phase also depends on global background data. We explain this dependence for ten-dimensional three-sphere bundles of quaternionic lines over closed spin seven-manifolds. The backgrounds retain two specified $\mathrm{Spin}(16)$ gauge bundles and an integral differential trivialization on the base, including its boundary identifications. The trivialized class is the sum of half the first Pontryagin class of the tangent bundle and the second Chern class of the quaternionic line's underlying complex bundle. For these backgrounds, the calculation combines a generally nonzero local Green-Schwarz integral with a real eta invariant evaluated by a twelve-dimensional Atiyah-Patodi-Singer filling. Together, these terms determine an integral relative characteristic number; the additive response is one third of that number, modulo integers. When the base's third and fourth rational cohomology groups vanish, the response equals eight times the Crowley-Goette invariant, without assuming that the base is simply connected. A calibration using the actual quaternionic line bundle gives additive value one third on $\mathrm{Sp}(2)$, recovering the known third-root phase. Distinct integral String data on one fixed ten-manifold give all three phase values, showing which global background information the response can detect. The physical identifications retain their prescribed fermion data, while the separate application to integral topological modular forms and vertex operator superalgebras requires compatible integral lifts. The response calculation constructs neither a microscopic realization nor an equivalence of complete theories.

A geometric Kähler--Ricci flow proof of the Calabi--Yau theorem
Authors: Bennett Chow, Yuan Liao
First: 2026-11-09T18:07:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-09T18:07:44+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We give a geometric proof that every Kähler class on a compact Kähler manifold with vanishing real first Chern class contains a unique Ricci-flat metric, and that the Kähler--Ricci flow converges smoothly to this metric. The fixed Kähler class and the bounded logarithmic volume density control the mean pairwise distance. Chow's scalar-curvature decay makes available, at a fixed spatial scale and all sufficiently late times, the relative-volume comparison of Jian--Song, derived from Bamler's pointed Nash entropy estimates. This comparison upgrades the mean-distance bound to a diameter bound without an absolute lower bound for entropy. A restarted heat-gradient estimate then gives exponential decay of the Ricci potential. A parabolic Schwarz estimate and the interior estimates of Sherman--Weinkove yield smooth exponential convergence. The proof does not invoke the Calabi--Yau existence theorem or global estimates for a scalar complex Monge--Ampère equation.

Abstract
Although $W^{2,2}(\mathbb{R}^{4})$ does not embed into $L^\infty$, we prove that radial symmetry allows the $L^\infty$ norm to be controlled by the homogeneous Sobolev seminorm $\dot H^2$, at the cost of a logarithmic correction involving a stronger Hölder-type norm. Abstract
We study isometric extensions from positive and full unit spheres of $C^\infty([0,1],\mathbb F)$, where $\mathbb F\in\{\mathbb R,\mathbb C\}$. We equip these spaces with a translation-invariant metric that induces the usual Fréchet topology, is unbounded, has convex balls, and has strictly increasing distances from zero along nonzero rays. Every surjective isometry of the pointwise positive unit sphere extends uniquely to a complex-linear surjective isometry of the complex-valued space. Restriction gives the unique real-linear isometric extension on the real-valued space. On the full unit sphere, every surjective isometry extends uniquely to a real-linear surjective isometry. The positive-sphere maps are compositions with the identity or an admissible reflection. The full-sphere maps also allow constant unimodular factors and, over the complex field, conjugation. We also classify the surjective ambient isometries. A counterexample shows that these metric properties alone do not guarantee positive-sphere extension. Under an explicit anchor condition, we classify surjective isometries of finite-order jet-positive spheres for maximum-type anchored metrics and prove that they extend uniquely to complex-linear surjective isometries. The classification allows any permutation of the anchored coordinates, combined with the identity or reflection on the highest-derivative component. The condition holds for every anchor when $m=1$ or $m=2$.

Abstract
We study a dense $N$-soliton condensate for the infinite Toda lattice in the limit $N \to \infty$. In contrast to previous mathematical studies of soliton gases where the norming constants are assumed to be deterministic or infinitesimal, we consider initial scattering data where both the discrete eigenvalues and the norming constants are independent random variables. The norming constants $γ_j$ are allowed to be arbitrarily large under a mild moment condition $\mathbb{E}[γ_j^ν] < \infty$ ($ν> 1$). Our main result is divided into two parts. First, we develop a probabilistic Riemann--Hilbert framework to show that the system self-averages: despite large, independent fluctuations in the initial data, the random Toda lattice variables converge deterministically to an effective continuum model at a polynomial rate $\mathcal{O}(N^{-δ})$ for $0 < δ< 1/2$. Second, by performing the Deift--Zhou nonlinear steepest descent analysis of the continuum problem, we obtain the complete leading-order macroscopic description of the lattice across the entire space-time plane. The solution decomposes into five distinct finite-gap regimes, characterized explicitly by modulated elliptic and genus-2 waves with an error of order $\mathcal{O}(1/\ln N)$. In contrast to the straight characteristic rays typical of standard soliton gas limits, the interfaces separating these five macroscopic regimes in the space-time plane are genuinely non-linear curves; in particular, the boundary of the modulated genus-2 region is completely non-linear. Finally, under our assumptions, we establish the Nonlinear Dispersion Relations for the Toda soliton condensate and prove that the macroscopic ensemble averages are determined by the soliton gas density of states.

Abstract
This paper investigates the solvability of infinite-dimensional differential-algebraic equations (DAEs) on Hilbert spaces. We focus on irregular systems for which the operator pencil is not surjective onto the full state space, but only onto a subspace determined by the algebraic constraints. We establish a Lumer-Phillips-type generation result for this class of systems, showing that dissipativity together with a partial surjectivity condition yields a contraction semigroup on the dynamic subspace. The resulting wellposedness conditions are compared with existing solvability results.
