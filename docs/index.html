First: 2026-10-15T17:57:39+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:57:39+00:00

Abstract
We construct a complete, second countable metric space with uncountable Scott rank. This answers a well-known question posed by Fokina, Friedman, K{ö}rwien and Nies, and shows that the known upper bound $ω_1$ on the rank, proved by Doucha, is sharp. Further, a Baire category based variant of the rank of metric spaces is developed, and this is used to give new proofs of the known rank bounds based only on standard methods of descriptive set theory.

Abstract
We study the moduli space of complete maximal surfaces in pseudo-hyperbolic space $\mathbf{H}^{2,2}$ with polygonal asymptotic boundary a lightlike $(n+4)$-gon in the Einstein universe $\mathbf{Ein}^{1,2}$, up to the action of $\mathsf{SO}_0(2,3)$. Importantly, this space $M_{n+4}(\mathbf{H}^{2,2})$ is disconnected for $n\geq 2$. We introduce a \emph{turning invariant} $Θ$ that classifies the components in terms of the geometry of the boundary polygons in the Einstein universe $\mathbf{Ein}^{1,2}$, which is related to \emph{spherical polygons} in $\mathbb{S}^2$. We provide a geometric parametrization of the moduli space $M_{n+4}(\mathbf{H}^{2,2})$ via holomorphic data, namely a pair of polynomials $α$, $β$. These polynomials appear in relation to certain components of the complexified second fundamental form of the corresponding surface. We then relate the holomorphic data $α,β$ with the turning invariant $Θ$. This leads to characterizations of the \emph{Hitchin component} of polygonal maximal surfaces studied by Tamburelli-Wolf.

Stratification of opers with apparent singularities and isomonodromic bubbling
Authors: Duong Dinh, Troy Figiel, Joerg Teschner
First: 2026-10-15T17:52:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:52:15+00:00
Comments: First version. Comments welcome!
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
On a compact Riemann surface of genus $g \geq 2$, we study Simpson's stratification of the de Rham moduli space of rank-2 flat connections. Upon representing flat $\mathrm{SL}_2(\mathbb{C})$-connections by branched projective structures, or equivalently by opers with apparent singularities, the stratum containing a flat connection is determined by the minimal number of apparent singularities needed to represent it. We construct explicit holomorphic families in which two simple apparent singularities collide and disappear, so that the family crosses into a smaller stratum; this realizes Simpson's closure relation between strata near generic points. The analogous families of Higgs bundles are described by Baker--Akhiezer divisors on the spectral curve in which two points converge to a pair of points exchanged by the spectral involution. We show that, before the collision, the two apparent singularities are joined by twin paths bounding a bubble, so that these families arise from the isomonodromic bubbling surgery discussed by Calsamiglia, Deroin and Francaviglia.

Abstract
We construct a deformation space for minimal immersions into $\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})$ with its holomorphic Killing form, and define an explicit, mapping class group invariant, complex hyperKähler structure on such deformation space. Abstract
We construct the first examples of one-dimensional long-range crystals exhibiting arbitrarily fast polynomial dispersive bounds. The Floquet functions of our Hamiltonians are highly oscillatory Weierstrass functions, whose rough autosimilar structure drives the fast dispersion. The proof develops a new Fourier decay theory for $C^α$ images of the Lebesgue measure, based on a Dolgopyat-inspired transfer operator method, and yields a van der Corput lemma for Weierstrass functions. As a consequence, the local time of classical Weierstrass functions of sufficiently large lacunarity exists and is $C^k$, answering a question raised by Geman and Horowitz in 1980.

Abstract
We introduce a new amenability property for étale groupoids, termed \textit{fiberwise amenability}, along with a stronger variant termed \emph{ubiquitous fiberwise amenability}. (Ubiquitous) fiberwise amenability emerges naturally from a coarse-geometric perspective on étale groupoids and, in the special case of transformation groupoids, it coincides precisely with the amenability of the acting group (rather than topological amenability of the action). It is also tightly linked to the existence of invariant measures on the unit space of the groupoid. The coarse-geometric framework for étale groupoids that we develop systematically in this work allows us to establish several foundational properties of (ubiquitous) fiberwise amenability. As an application, we prove a Følner--paradoxical dichotomy for minimal étale groupoids, which will serve as a key tool in a sequel on almost elementariness of étale groupoids.

Abstract
We solve two long outstanding problems here. We first solve in ZFC a problem that the first author and Weaver posed ten years ago: a state on a von Neumann algebra is countably additive on orthogonal projections if and only if it is sequentially weak* continuous, equivalently, sequentially normal. Such states may be considered the quantum (countably additive) probability measures, or rather their extension to a noncommutative integral. The principal tools are known ideas from direct integral theory from the 1970s. Indeed we prove a disintegration theorem for states on products of sigma-finite von Neumann algebras. We give several applications. For example we give a new variant of Gleason theorem, extending countably additive probability measures on projections to sequentially weak* continuous states when the algebra has no type $I_2$ direct summand. We also establish normality and sequential normality criteria for weights, and give several other applications of our main result to weights. In forthcoming work we consider many applications to `quantum measure and integration theory', such as variants of Lebesgue's dominated convergence theorem for von Neumann algebras. The analytic tools and product-quotient construction developed here are used to prove in an Appendix by the first author that normality and sequential normality of arbitrary weights are determined by their restrictions to abelian von Neumann subalgebras, resolving Haagerup's Problem 1.11. (This may be moved to a companion paper.) That is, normality of weights is commutatively determined.

Abstract
We give a quantitative planar form of a multiscale argument for Falconer's distance problem. For every $0<η<1$, we obtain a positive lower bound for the Lebesgue measure of the distance set of a compact subset of the unit ball supporting a probability measure with $ν(B(x,r))\le 2r^{1+η}$. The bound depends only on $η$. For a compact set of diameter $D$ carrying a probability measure with $μ(B(x,r))\le A(r/D)^{1+\varepsilon}$, localization gives $|ΔE|\ge c_{\varepsilon,p}DA^{-p}$ for every fixed $p>1/\varepsilon$. In particular, compact planar sets of Hausdorff dimension greater than one have distance sets of positive Lebesgue measure. The proof develops the multiscale method of the September 2026 manuscript of OpenAI, using one sequence of restricted distance measures for both the mass and Fourier estimates. A reconstruction lemma combines summable total-variation errors with summable squared Fourier norms. No endpoint or sharpness assertion is made.

Abstract
It is proved that every Borel ensemble of pure states on a separable bipartite Hilbert space with finite average entanglement admits a countable ensemble with the same barycenter and arbitrarily small additional average entanglement. Consequently, the discrete and continuous versions of the Entanglement of Formation coincide on the whole space of states of infinite-dimensional bipartite quantum system (with infinite values allowed). The main idea of the proof (realized with the AI-assistance) is the lifting a Borel ensemble of pure bipartite states to a Borel probability measure on the unit sphere in the space of Hilbert--Schmidt operators followed by dividing the support of this measure into appropriate cells and replacing each cell by its conditional barycenter. The resulting countable ensemble is obtained by a recursive construction.

Abstract
To solve a large class of noncompact problems ubiquitous in random functional analysis and its applications, most of compactness-based fundamental theorems established for normed spaces or locally convex spaces are required to be extended for random normed modules and random locally convex modules in an appropriate way of weakening compactness. Abstract
Let $T$ be the tetragonal disphenoid whose four congruent faces are isosceles triangles with sides $2,\sqrt3,\sqrt3$, so that the dihedral angles are $π/2$ along one opposite pair of edges and $π/3$ along the remaining four. By Smyth's theorem $T$ contains three embedded minimal disks meeting $\partial T$ orthogonally; the symmetry group $D_{2d}$ of $T$ fixes one and interchanges the other two. We identify all three completely. The distinguished disk is a patch of $\textbf{Schwarz's $\mathrm{P}$ surface}$ and the congruent pair are patches of $\textbf{Schwarz's $\mathrm{D}$ surface}$, with exact areas \[ \operatorname{Area}(Σ^{*}_{A}) = \frac{K\!\left(\tfrac14\right)} {K\!\left(\tfrac34\right)} = 0.781700961348056\ldots, \] \[ \operatorname{Area}(Σ^{*}_{B}) = \frac{K\!\left(\tfrac34\right)} {2\,K\!\left(\tfrac14\right)} = 0.639630785585503\ldots \] in the normalization $\operatorname{Vol}(T)=2/3$, inradius $r_T=\sqrt2/4$. In particular $2\,\operatorname{Area}(Σ^{*}_{A})\operatorname{Area}(Σ^{*}_{B})=1$, which we show is the Bonnet relation for the adjoint pair $(\mathrm{D},\mathrm{P})$. The proof is a reversal of the usual strategy: rather than uniformizing the Gauss map, we identify Smyth's three Plateau contours as the $\textit{tetrahedral four edges}$ and the $\textit{octahedral four edges}$ --- classically solved by $\mathrm{D}$ and $\mathrm{P}$ respectively --- and then conjugate. Both areas agree with Surface Evolver computations of Ken Brakke to all $15$ reported digits. We also show that all twelve corners of the three disks are midpoints of edges of $T$, each of the six midpoints being used twice, and that $T$ is precisely the alcove of the affine Weyl group $\widetilde{A}_{3}$, confirming a remark of Gandy, Cvijović, et al. (1999).

Abstract
For any translation invariant generalized complex structure J on a torus we construct the derived category D(J) generalizing the derived category of coherent sheaves on a complex torus and the affine/convergent Fukaya category of a symplectic torus.
