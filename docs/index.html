The Cauchy problem for skew-torsion-parallel spinors with applications to supergravity
Authors: C. S. Shahbazi
First: 2026-11-12T18:58:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-12T18:58:38+00:00
Comments: 65 pages. Comments welcome!
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We solve the Cauchy problem for an irreducible real spinor parallel with respect to a metric connection with skew-symmetric torsion on a globally hyperbolic Lorentzian four-manifold, proving that it is well-posed modulo an obstruction that we identify explicitly, and obtaining an initial-data characterization of such spinors. Our proof relies on the theory of spinorial forms, which we use to explicitly integrate the coupled flow for the spinor and underlying metric via a rotated pull-back action of the flow generated by a vector field on the Cauchy hypersurface $Σ$. We characterize the corresponding constraint equations as an explicit exterior differential system and describe all its solutions in terms of contact structures and flat foliations, obtaining a complete classification in the compact case. In particular, we conclude that every oriented compact three-manifold arises as the Cauchy hypersurface of a globally hyperbolic Lorentzian four-manifold equipped with a skew-torsion parallel spinor. Building on these results, we study the interaction between the four-dimensional Neveu-Schwarz supergravity evolution flow on an abelian bundle gerbe and the spinorial evolution flow determined by its supersymmetric conditions, which we prove to be well-posed and compatible. This establishes the well-posedness of the supersymmetric Neveu-Schwarz system in four dimensions and provides an initial-data characterization of supersymmetry. Furthermore, we obtain a strong rigidity result stating that every globally hyperbolic supersymmetric Neveu-Schwarz solution with a compact Cauchy hypersurface is flat and has vanishing bundle-gerbe curvature.

Abstract
We construct smooth isometric embeddings of closed surfaces of positive genus in Euclidean 3-space which are tight, i.e., have minimal total positive curvature, but are not congruent. These embeddings agree outside a compact subset of the region of negative curvature, and enclose different volumes. Thus the rigidity theorem of Alexandrov for analytic tight surfaces does not hold in the smooth category, which settles problems of Nirenberg and Banchoff-Kuhnel.

Stability for the Bray-Brendle-Neves Scalar Sphere Rigidity Theorem
Authors: Carolina Ruviaro
First: 2026-11-12T18:44:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-12T18:44:06+00:00

Abstract
Let $(M^3,g)$ be a closed and oriented 3-dimensional Riemannian manifold with $π_2(M)\neq 0$ and let $A_{π_2}(M,g)$ denote the least area among homotopically nontrivial two-spheres in $M$. By a theorem of Bray-Brendle-Neves, $A_{π_2}(M,g)\inf_M R_g \leq 8π$ with equality characterizing the cylindrical geometry $S^2\times\mathbb{R}$. Motivated by Sormani's questions on volume preserving intrinsic flat stability for scalar curvature rigidity problems, we study the almost equality regime of this theorem. We build, using Sweeney's tunnel construction, a sequence satisfying $A_{π_2}(M_j,g_j)\inf_{M_j} R_{g_j} \rightarrow 8π$ with uniformly bounded volume and diameter whose volume preserving intrinsic flat limit is not the Bray-Brendle-Neves rigidity space, showing that stability fails in general. We then prove stability in two special cases: for sequences of rotationally symmetric metrics (with non-collapsing assumptions) and for sequences of manifolds with nonnegative Ricci curvature.

Abstract
We construct Schauder bases of $L^p(\mathbb{R})$ with significant structural constraints on their elements. For every $1 \leq p< \infty$, we obtain bases of non-negative functions with basis constants arbitrarily close to one, as well as bases with positive biorthogonal functionals. Abstract
We establish optimal sheet separation estimates for stable $s$-minimal hypersurfaces in dimensions up to seven, using an iteration argument in the nonlocal approximation theory of Chan-Dipierro-Serra-Valdinoci. These estimates imply that every stable $s$-minimal hypersurface in $\mathbb{R}^7$ is a hyperplane when $s$ is close to $1$. More generally, every finite Morse index $s$-minimal set in $\mathbb{R}^7$ is a half-space. We also establish a compactness theorem for stable $s$-minimal cones in $\mathbb{R}^8$. We prove uniform curvature and area estimates for their links and show that, as $s \uparrow 1$, they subconverge graphically in $C^{2,α}$, either with multiplicity one to a classical stable minimal cone, or with multiplicity two to a hyperplane. The stability of Lawson and axisymmetric $s$-minimal cones in $\mathbb{R}^8$ shows that these results are optimal.

Patterns, Distances, and Structure in Sparse Sets
Authors: Paige Bright
First: 2026-11-12T18:01:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-12T18:01:28+00:00
Comments: 30 pages, 3 figures. To appear in the Springer volume 'Discrete Harmonic Analysis' in the Bolyai Society Lecture Notes Series
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A central theme in modern harmonic analysis, with strong connections to Ramsey theory, is the tension between randomness and structure: to what extent can a "large" set avoid prescribed configurations, and when must such patterns inevitably appear? The answer depends delicately on how size is measured, with notions such as Hausdorff dimension, Fourier dimension, and density leading to strikingly different phenomena. In this mini-course, we explore this theme through two complementary perspectives. On one hand, we study translation-invariant linear patterns, a natural class of Euclidean Ramsey-type configurations. On the other hand, we investigate distance sets of sparse subsets of Euclidean space, which encode metric configurations of pairs of points. Together, these results illustrate a unifying principle: while sparse sets may avoid individual configurations, sufficiently strong analytic or geometric conditions enforce the emergence of Ramsey-type structure. The course will emphasize key ideas, methods, and examples, aiming to provide an accessible entry point to current developments at the interface of harmonic analysis, geometric measure theory, and Euclidean Ramsey theory. Lectures are by Malabika Pramanik.

Spinorial geometry of Bismut--Hermitian--Einstein manifolds
Authors: Beatrice Brienza, Matteo Kevin Crisafio, Anna Fino, Dario Martelli
First: 2026-11-12T17:59:20+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-12T17:59:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We present a pair of spinorial equations, generalizing the internal Killing spinor equations of Type I supergravity, and show that they characterize several classes of Bismut-Hermitian-Einstein structures on six-dimensional Riemannian spin manifolds. We show that in this framework the local reduction to a four-dimensional transverse Hermitian geometry is provided by two canonical, commuting, Bismut-parallel Killing vector fields arising as bilinears in the Killing spinors, without assuming compactness. In one class, the transverse geometry is conformally Kahler and both compact and non-compact solutions exist. We also discuss variants that do not admit compact solutions, one of which has hyper-Hermitian transverse geometry. We discuss the relation of these structures to the Hull-Strominger system with closed three-form and to five-dimensional Gauntlett-Kim geometries and provide explicit embeddings in Type I, Type IIB and massive Type IIA supergravity, as well as in generalized supergravity. We illustrate our results with a number of examples, both compact and non-compact.

Abstract
We complete the classification of algebraic relations among solutions of equations in the second Painlevé family $P_{II}(α): y''=2y^3+ty+α$, where $α\in\mathbb{C}$. More precisely, we prove that if $y_1,\ldots,y_n$ are generic solutions of $P_{II}(α_1),\ldots,P_{II}(α_n)$, respectively, and $tr.deg_{\mathbb{C}(t)}\mathbb{C}(t)(y_1,y_1',\ldots,y_n,y_n')<2n$, then for some $i<j$, we have that $α_i\pmα_j\in\mathbb{Z}$, and $y_i=T(y_j)$ for a classical Bäcklund transformation $T$. Frame constructions associated with operator orbits
Authors: Eva A. Gallardo-Gutiérrez, Jonathan R. Partington
First: 2026-05-28T09:33:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-12T17:44:41+00:00
Comments: 26 pages, paper totally rewritten with several new results (see the abstract)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
This paper studies frames in Hilbert spaces generated by the orbits of finitely many vectors under a single operator, presenting new results on normal operators and multiplication operators as well as on operators composed of Jordan blocks. These results generalize existing results of Cabrelli, Molter, Paternostro and Philipp by means of techniques which deal with weighted interpolation, weighted composition operators, and Beurling--Lax theory related to shifts of finite multiplicity. Likewise, we discuss Carleson frames and construct counterexamples to a recent conjecture of Aldroubi, Cabrelli, Krishtal and Molter, showing that the relevant necessary condition is of density type rather than the Müntz--Szász condition. Moreover, by using the theory of weighted composition operators, we answer a question of Cheshmavar on frames produced by iterative multiplication. Finally, for orbits of infinitely many vectors the underlying model is a shift of infinite multiplicity which, by a theorem of Rota, is a universal operator; consequently, a complete description of such frames along these lines would encompass the Invariant Subspace Problem.

Abstract
In this paper, we show that for every p>2, there is an orthonormal basis of spherical harmonics on the n-dimensional unit sphere which achieves the maximal Lp norm growth. More precisely, one basis achieves this growth for p up to the critical exponent 2(n+1)/(n-1), and another for p from the critical exponent up to infinity. The first basis is built from rotations of the real part of a highest weight spherical harmonic. The second is built from zonal harmonics whose poles are selected from a spherical design. In both cases, the selection uses the restricted invertibility theorem of Bourgain and Tzafriri. This extends earlier results of Bourgain and of the author (arXiv:1404.5016) on the 2-dimensional sphere for 2<p<=6 to all dimensions and all p>2, and improves the positive density subsequence there to a whole basis.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study recovery of an initial state from dynamical samples of a trajectory driven by unknown additive forcing. Measurements against both the original sensors and their images under the adjoint nominal evolution yield one-step residuals that cancel the initial state exactly. These residuals separate forcing estimation from initial-state reconstruction. For a centered Gaussian forcing model with fixed covariance parameters, we combine Gaussian conditioning with canonical dual-frame reconstruction and derive an exact unconditional second-moment identity, including the correlation between the forcing-estimation error and the measurement noise. We also obtain high-probability bounds and identify the irreducible forcing uncertainty through a conditional covariance in the geometry induced by the prior. A hierarchy of augmented sensor systems separates the role of sampling depth in forcing visibility from that of the observation horizon in nominal observability. An image-transport experiment and finite-dimensional and periodic-convolution experiments illustrate the residual correction and the separate roles of sampling depth and observation horizon.

Comments: 63 pages

Abstract
The quantitative study of the Gaussian measure of symmetric convex sets is a central theme at the interface of probability theory and convex geometry. From a probabilistic perspective, it amounts to analyzing the tail behavior of suprema of Gaussian processes. The typical magnitude and upper tail of Gaussian processes are well-understood, thanks to Talagrand's celebrated Majorizing Measures theorem and to Gaussian concentration. In contrast, despite fundamental connections with metric entropy established by Kuelbs and Li, no general sharp characterization of the lower tail is available. In this work, we close this gap by establishing a sharp two-sided characterization of Gaussian small ball probabilities, involving both metric entropy and local widths of the underlying convex body. This yields an explicit chaining-type description of lower quantiles of Gaussian processes in terms of the metric structure of the convexified index set, as well as a general comparison inequality for convex sets. We further obtain analogous quantitative characterizations of two important geometric quantities associated with symmetric convex sets, namely intrinsic volumes (quermassintegrals) and the radii of random sections beyond the classical Dvoretzky range. A unifying theme of these results is the central role of convexity in the quantitative understanding of small ball probabilities.

The Large-$N$ Limit of Ricci curvature in the Zeitlin Model on the Torus
Authors: Leandro Lichtenfelz, Isabelle Raad, Justin Valletta
First: 2026-11-02T00:15:45+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-12T16:38:06+00:00
Comments: 25 pages, 1 figure

Abstract
The Euler equations of hydrodynamics describe the motion of an ideal incompressible fluid. Arnold observed that their solutions are geodesics on the group of volume-preserving diffeomorphisms of the fluid domain, equipped with the $L^2$ metric, so that the curvature of this group governs how nearby fluid motions separate. Sectional curvature takes both signs, and Ricci curvature, its average over all directions, is the natural measure of which sign prevails, but in infinite dimensions the sum defining it diverges. When the fluid domain is the flat torus, we define Ricci curvature through Zeitlin's finite-dimensional models, which approximate the Euler equations by geodesic equations on the matrix groups $SU(N)$. We compute the Ricci curvature of these models exactly and prove that, normalized by dimension, it converges as $N \rightarrow \infty$ to an explicit integral. The limit is strictly negative in every Fourier mode and decreases logarithmically with the wavenumber, the behavior conjectured in \cite{lichtenfelz2026ricci} for the sphere. We also show that the diameter of $SU(N)$ in the Zeitlin metric grows at most linearly in $N$.

Abstract
We construct an outer function in the classical Dirichlet space whose radial zero set has logarithmic capacity zero, but whose polynomial multiples do not approximate the constant function $1$. This disproves the Brown--Shields conjecture. The proof rests on two approximation theorems for canonical generators of invariant subspaces. The first shows that every proper outer canonical generator can be approximated in the Dirichlet norm by canonical generators associated with singular inner functions determined by finite positive atomic measures. The second shows that each fixed finite-atomic canonical generator can be recovered as a strong Dirichlet limit of outer canonical generators whose radial zero sets have arbitrarily small logarithmic capacity. Abstract
Arranging points in the plane appears to be an elementary geometric problem, yet it is deeply rooted in statistical physics. As a toy model, we consider the configuration of four points. Abstract
Fréchet means are a popular type of average for non-Euclidean datasets, defined as those points that minimise the average squared distance to a set of data points. We consider the behaviour of sample Fréchet means on normed spaces whose unit ball is a polytope; this setting is particularly important for semi-discrete data, but is rarely covered by existing literature on Fréchet means, which focuses primarily on manifolds and spaces with bounded Alexandrov curvature. We study the geometry of the set of Fréchet means over polytope normed spaces, with a focus on dimension and probabilistic conditions for uniqueness. In particular, we provide a geometric characterisation of the threshold sample size at which Fréchet means have a positive probability of being unique, and we prove that this threshold is at most one more than the dimension of our space. This geometric characterisation presents a novel framework for computing the unique Fréchet mean sample threshold, which we demonstrate in the case of the $\ell_1$, $\ell_{\infty}$ and tropical norms. In particular, we show that the $\ell_1$ sample threshold is always 3, but the $\ell_{\infty}$ and tropical sample thresholds grow at different rates with the dimension of our space.
