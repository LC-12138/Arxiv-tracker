The Singular Fibration of Collapsing RCD Spaces with Reifenberg Local Covers
Authors: Shaosai Huang
First: 2026-10-12T18:01:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:53:46+00:00
Comments: Updated version of the original paper titled "On the collapsing of spaces with local rewinding Reifenberg control" with new results along the same line of research; no separate submission of the results due to arxiv monthly submission restriction
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Let compact $\mathrm{RCD}(κ,N)$ spaces converge in the measured Gromov--Hausdorff sense, and suppose that they have $(δ,ρ)$-Reifenberg local covers for one fixed $ρ>0$, with $|κ|ρ^2\le1$ and $δ$ small. We prove that, along a subsequence, the spaces are singular fibrations over the limit: their canonical frame spaces converge to a topological manifold with a locally linear action of $O(n)$ whose quotient is the limit; for large indices the frame spaces fibre over this manifold, by maps that commute with $O(n)$, with fibres homeomorphic to nilmanifolds; and the induced maps to the limit are, near every point, quotients of products by the isotropy group, with fibres homeomorphic to infranilmanifolds. As applications, we extend a fibration theorem of Honda and Zhang to non-smooth RCD spaces whose local covers have injectivity radius bounded below, and we show that for aspherical spaces the isotropy groups are finite, the fibre groups inject into the fundamental groups, and the limit is the quotient of a contractible manifold by a proper cocompact action; under Rong's condition $A_s$ it is a closed aspherical manifold. Three appendices treat smooth manifolds with $\mathrm{Ric}\ge-(n-1)$ and small local rewinding Reifenberg error: a uniform short-time Ricci flow with $|\mathrm{Rm}|\leα/t$, also for complete non-compact data, where it is unique among complete flows with curvature at most $A/t$; a second construction with affine nilmanifold bundles; and, conditional on Nifa's construction, examples showing that rewinding volume control alone does not give continuous Gromov--Hausdorff approximations with small error.

A category of locally convex Lie algebroids
Authors: Ahmed Gamal Shaltut
First: 2026-07-08T17:25:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T17:40:19+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study first-order locally convex Lie algebroids in the setting of Bastiani calculus. The first-order condition is automatic in finite rank, but is an additional regularity hypothesis for general locally convex vector bundles. We define sheaves of scalar-valued and vector-valued Lie algebroid forms as fiberwise continuous alternating maps with smooth local representatives. Morphisms are defined by requiring the induced pullback on inverse-image sheaves of scalar-valued forms to commute with the Lie algebroid differentials. We prove that these algebroids form a category, study representations and cohomology sheaves, and show that the Lie algebroids and morphisms of locally convex Lie groupoids belong to this category. We also give two applications. For a compact manifold $K$, the current algebroid $C^\infty(K,A)\to C^\infty(K,M)$ of a first-order Banach Lie algebroid $A\to M$ is a first-order Fréchet Lie algebroid, provided $M$ admits local addition. Finally, we prove a Banach Lie II theorem: a morphism from the Lie algebroid of a locally convex Lie groupoid to that of a Banach-Lie groupoid, both with Hausdorff source fibers, integrates uniquely to a groupoid morphism when the source groupoid is source-connected and source-simply connected.

Local crystallization in three-dimensional quantization

Abstract
A long-standing conjecture on the quantization problem due to Gersho, which remains open in dimensions higher than two, posits that optimal quantizers crystallize into a lattice structure; in dimension three, the natural candidate is the body-centered cubic (BCC) lattice, known to be optimal among other lattices. In this paper, we establish the asymptotic local optimality of the BCC lattice under small periodic perturbations, extending its known stability beyond the class of lattice configurations. Our approach connects the discrete geometry of Voronoi tessellations with a continuum variational problem, showing that the rescaled quadratic quantization energies converge to a continuum functional describing locally deformed BCC lattices. A central ingredient is a geometric and algebraic framework that combines the stability of the BCC Voronoi structure, a discrete calculus on its adjacency graph and an analysis of the lattice symmetries via representation theory, allowing us to establish the positivity of the second variation at the BCC configuration and thereby prove the local minimality of the BCC lattice for the limiting energy.

Authors: Ekaterina Amerik, Andrey Soldatenkov, Misha Verbitsky

Comments: 64 pages, version 2.1, refereed version to be published in Advances in Math
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The ample cone of a compact Kahler $n$-manifold $M$ is the intersection of its Kahler cone and the real subspace generated by integer (1,1)-classes. Its isotropic boundary is the set of all points $η$ on its boundary such that $\int_M η^n=0$. We are interested in the relation between the shape of the isotropic boundary of the ample cone of a hyperkahler manifold and the dynamics of its holomorphic automorphism group $G$. In this case, the projectivization of the ample cone is realized as an open, locally polyhedral subset in a hyperbolic space $H$. The isotropic boundary $S$ is realized as a subset of the hyperbolic boundary (the absolute) $A$ of $H$, which is naturally identified with a Euclidean sphere. It is clear that the isotropic boundary $S$ contains the limit set of $G$ acting on its ample cone. We prove that, conversely, all irrational points on $S$ belong to the limit set. Using a result of N. Shah about limiting distributions of curves under geodesic flow on hyperbolic manifolds, we prove that every real analytic curve in $S$ is contained in a geodesic sphere in $S$,and in presence of such curves the limit set is the closure of the union of these geodesic spheres. We study the geometry of such fractal sets, called Apollonian carpets, and establish the link between the Apollonian carpet and the structure of the automorphism group.

Abstract
Correlation matrices of bounded rank form a stratified space whose factor representations lie in a product of spheres. Using the orbit-type description of the rank stratification, we prove that padding factors preserves the Riemannian geometry of each fixed-rank stratum and the intrinsic length metric of every rank-bounded subset. In the reduced model, a Sylvester equation describes the mechanical connection and its horizontal lifts. Abstract
We prove Gromov's hyperbolic hyperspherical radius comparison and rigidity conjectures in all dimensions $n\ge3$, without the spin assumption. Abstract
Let $θ$ be a nonconstant inner function, we prove that every bounded truncated Toeplitz operator on the model space $K_θ=H^2\ominusθH^2$ admits a bounded symbol if and only if $θ$ is one-component, that is, if $\{z\in\mathbb D:|θ(z)|<r\}$ is nonempty and connected for some $r\in(0,1)$. This establishes the previously open necessary direction and resolves a conjecture of Baranov, Chalendar, Fricain, Mashreghi, and Timotin. For every non-one-component inner function, we construct a sequence of norm-one rank-one truncated Toeplitz operators whose infimal $L^\infty$-norms over all representing symbols tend to infinity. The quantitative lower bound is obtained by forming two inner divisors from angularly separated parts of the zero set and singular measure. The argument applies without restrictions on the Blaschke or singular parts of $θ$. The order of this lower bound is sharp, already for a family of degree-two Blaschke products. A continuation argument for local inverse branches produces the required sequence of points. Consequently, every non-one-component model space supports a trace-class truncated Toeplitz operator without a bounded symbol. Combined with known equivalences, the characterization also yields geometric criteria for weak factorization and for continuous-symbol representations of compact truncated Hankel operators.

Abstract
We prove a sharp linear upper bound for the interior nodal length of Steklov eigenfunctions on smooth compact Riemannian surfaces with boundary, establishing the upper bound predicted by the Steklov analogue of Yau's conjecture in dimension two. On each fixed surface, we also show that the nodal length divided by the logarithm of the eigenvalue tends to infinity, uniformly over the eigenfunctions. We construct examples to show this divergence can be arbitrarily slow. Moreover, boundary zero counts and both interior and boundary nodal domain counts satisfy sharp linear bounds for large eigenvalues. We also prove density estimates for boundary zeros on at least one boundary component and construct a fixed analytic cylinder on which another component remains free of boundary zeros along an unbounded sequence of eigenvalues.

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Riemannian manifolds are powerful models for problems with non-Euclidean structure. Recent work in geometric machine learning has focused on training deep neural networks to learn Riemannian metrics from high-dimensional data. However, many geometric algorithms require repeated evaluations of the exponential and logarithmic maps, whose high computational cost restricts the utility of learned Riemannian manifolds in practice. We propose to address this cost by training a conditional invertible neural network, called a flow chart, to approximate normal coordinates around points on a given Riemannian manifold. Abstract
We establish geometric and analytic rigidity results for the Gagliardo-Nirenberg equation. On a complete noncompact Riemannian manifold with nonnegative Ricci curvature, the existence of a positive critical point of the Gagliardo-Nirenberg quotient forces the manifold to be Euclidean and the solution to be extremal. No minimizing assumption or volume growth hypothesis is required, thereby strengthening earlier rigidity results. In Euclidean space, we prove that all positive solutions of the normalized equation whose nonlinear mass on expanding annuli has sublinear growth are extremals. Kadison duality for biaffinely convex sets
Authors: Tea Štrekelj
First: 2026-10-20T13:45:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-20T13:45:38+00:00
Comments: 22 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Classical Kadison duality establishes a categorical equivalence between compact convex sets and Archimedean order unit spaces of affine functions. This paper develops a Kadison-type duality for sets with coordinate-wise convex structure in product domains $\mathbb{R}^n \times \mathbb{R}^m$. While slice-wise convex (biconvex) sets appear to be the natural geometric domain, separate convexity along coordinates is insufficient to ensure separation by biaffine polynomials. In classical convexity, every closed convex set is determined by affine functionals because any exterior point can be separated by a hyperplane. In contrast, biaffine polynomials can only probe a biconvex set $K$ up to its biaffine hull biconv$(K)$, the envelope formed by the intersection of all regions cut out by valid biaffine inequalities on $K$. To resolve this obstruction, the geometric setting is restricted to biaffinely convex sets, those that coincide with their biaffine hull ($K =$ biconv$(K)$) and therefore admit strict biaffine separation. On the algebraic side, we introduce the dual objects, which we call biaffine order unit spaces. These are finite-dimensional Archimedean order unit spaces defined on tensor products $E_1 \otimes E_2$ whose order units factor and whose positive cones are governed entirely by unentangled, rank-1 tensor evaluations. We prove a duality between the category of compact non-degenerate biaffinely convex sets with coordinate-wise affine maps and the category of abstract biaffine order unit spaces with tensor-factorized unital positive linear maps. In particular, every such biaffinely convex set is isomorphic to the coordinate state space of its biaffine polynomials, and conversely, every biaffine order unit space is order-isomorphic to the space of biaffine polynomials on its coordinate state space.
